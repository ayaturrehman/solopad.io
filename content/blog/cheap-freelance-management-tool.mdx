A cheap freelance tool is useful when its plan covers the work you need. Start by listing the subscriptions you pay for, then mark which tasks each one handles.

Keep the services that do something essential. Look for overlap in proposals, contracts, invoices, scheduling and time tracking. Replacing overlapping subscriptions can simplify your admin, but the saving depends on your actual plans and usage.

I build SoloPad. This guide explains how to compare costs; it is not a report of verified customer savings or a personal bank-statement analysis.

## Build a fair comparison

For each option, record:

- The subscription price and currency.
- Monthly billing versus an annual upfront payment.
- The number of paid users or contributors.
- The plan required for your essential features.
- Client payment, usage and optional add-on charges.

Use the vendors' current pricing pages, linked in our [freelance software shortlist](/blog/best-freelance-management-software-2026). Avoid comparing one product's annual equivalent with another product's rolling monthly price.

## Work out a realistic saving

Suppose your existing subscriptions cost **£40/month** and you need time tracking and scheduling. Comparing that stack with SoloPad Solo at **£12/month** gives a subscription difference of **£28/month**, or **£336 over twelve monthly payments**.

That is an illustrative calculation, not a customer result. It excludes payment fees, taxes, currency movements and services you still need. Use the same method with your own figures. If you only need Starter's features, compare Starter separately.

For client payments of £1,000 through SoloPad Starter, a 3% platform fee is £30 before Stripe processing fees. Include that in the budget rather than assuming a £5 subscription is the total cost.

## Keep tools that still earn their place

Your accounting package may serve a different purpose from client management. A specialist scheduling setup may also be worth keeping. Compare the time and functionality you would lose before cancelling either.

During a trial, prepare a proposal and invoice, check the client view, and test an export. Confirm the replacement handles your real work before migrating records or ending another subscription.
