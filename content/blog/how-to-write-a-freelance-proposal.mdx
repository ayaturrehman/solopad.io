A useful freelance proposal helps the client understand what you will deliver, what it will cost and how to proceed. Keep those decisions easy to find.

This guide gives you a structure and illustrative wording to adapt. It does not promise a particular reply or acceptance rate.

## Start with the client's problem

Use the opening to show that you understand the brief. Describe the outcome the client needs and the constraint that matters most.

For an illustrative booking-website project, you could write:

> You need clients to find available appointments and book without a phone call. I propose a five-page website connected to your existing booking service, with a clear route from the homepage to an appointment.

Only claim relevant experience or past results if you can substantiate them.

## Define the deliverables and exclusions

List what the price includes. Name the number of pages, design concepts, revisions or other units where that helps make the scope clear.

Explain what is excluded and what the client must supply. If content, access or feedback is needed before delivery, make that dependency visible. A [scope of work template](/blog/freelance-scope-of-work-template) can help you organise the detail.

## Explain how you will approach the work

Give the client a short sequence: discovery, first draft, review and delivery. Include the points at which they need to make a decision.

Keep technical detail proportional to the job. A proposal should help the client approve the work; supporting specifications can sit in a separate document.

## State the price and payment schedule

For example, a fictional £2,400 project could use a £1,200 deposit and £1,200 on the agreed completion milestone. Explain the payment trigger, due date and any applicable tax treatment.

If the scope is still uncertain, explain the assumptions behind an estimate or offer a paid discovery stage. Avoid presenting an unbounded range as a fixed quote.

Use your own [freelance rates](/blog/how-to-set-freelance-rates-uk). A writing tool cannot decide what your work should cost.

## Show relevant evidence

Choose one or two examples that relate to the client's problem. Explain your role and link to work you have permission to share. Label sample or fictional projects clearly.

Check the evidence before sending. A plausible case study generated by AI is not proof of experience.

## End with a specific next step

Explain how the client can ask questions, approve the proposal and move to the contract or deposit stage. For example:

> If the scope and price work for you, reply with your approval by Friday. I will then send the agreement and deposit invoice. We can confirm the start date once those are complete.

Adapt the steps to your actual process. Approval should not silently trigger obligations the client has not seen.
