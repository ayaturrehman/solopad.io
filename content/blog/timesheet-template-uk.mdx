This is when you need a **timesheet** — not a fancy time-tracking app (though those help), not a vague "I worked on your project this week" message, but a clear, client-facing record that shows what you did, when you did it, and how long it took.

## What a timesheet is (and when UK freelancers need one)

A **timesheet** is a record of hours worked on a project or for a client, usually broken down by date and task. It shows what you did, when you did it, and how many hours it took.

Timesheets are used for:

✓ **Hourly or day-rate billing** — Client pays for time, not deliverables  
✓ **Retainer agreements** — Client has a monthly hour bank and you track against it  
✓ **Client disputes** — Proof of hours if the client questions your invoice  
✓ **VAT and tax records** — Supporting evidence for HMRC if your income is queried  
✓ **Internal tracking** — Understanding where your time goes (billable vs admin)  

### Timesheet vs invoice — what is the difference?

A **timesheet** is the backup. It shows what you did and how long it took.

An **invoice** is the payment request. It states the amount due, payment terms, and bank details.

For hourly or day-rate work, you attach the timesheet to the invoice as proof of hours. The timesheet says "I worked 12.5 hours this week on X tasks." The invoice says "You owe me £625. Please pay within 14 days."

For fixed-price projects, you do not need a timesheet. The invoice just says "Website redesign as per contract: £3,000."

**Tip:** If you track time on fixed-price projects for your own records (to see if you are profitable), keep that internal. Do not send it to the client unless they specifically ask for proof of effort.

## When UK freelancers need a timesheet — the decision tree

Not every project needs a timesheet. Here is when to use one and when to skip it.

### You NEED a timesheet when:

✓ **You bill hourly or day-rate** — The client is paying for your time, so you must prove the hours  
✓ **You have a retainer with tracked hours** — The client has a 20-hour/month bank and you need to show what you used  
✓ **The client asks for time breakdowns** — Some corporates or agencies require timesheets even for fixed-price work (common in legal, consulting, or government projects)  
✓ **The project is long and scope is fluid** — Tracking hours helps you spot scope creep and renegotiate if the client keeps adding tasks  

### You can SKIP a timesheet when:

✓ **You bill fixed-price** — The client agreed to £3,000 for a logo. They do not need to know it took you 8 hours or 18 hours.  
✓ **The project is a one-off milestone** — You delivered the work, they pay the agreed amount. No time breakdown needed.  
✓ **The client never asks for one** — If your invoice is clear and the client pays without question, do not add extra paperwork.  

**Bottom line:** If the client is paying for time (hourly, day-rate, retainer), send a timesheet with every invoice. If the client is paying for deliverables (fixed-price), skip the timesheet unless they ask for it.

## What to include on a UK freelance timesheet — field checklist

A client-facing timesheet should be simple, professional, and impossible to misinterpret. Here is what to include.

### Required fields

✓ **Your name and business name** (if you have a limited company)  
✓ **Client name**  
✓ **Invoice reference or period** (e.g. "Invoice INV-2026-042" or "Week ending 27 September 2026")  
✓ **Date** (day of the week helps — "Monday 23 Sept" is clearer than "23/09/2026")  
✓ **Task or project description** (what you worked on)  
✓ **Hours worked** (decimal format: 2.5 not 2:30)  
✓ **Rate** (GBP hourly or day rate)  
✓ **Line total** (rate × hours)  
✓ **Total hours and total amount** at the bottom  

### Helpful optional fields

✓ **Start and end times** (if the client requires proof you worked specific hours — common for contractors working set shifts)  
✓ **Notes column** (brief context for each task)  
✓ **Retainer balance** (hours remaining if on a monthly hour bank)  

**Do NOT include:**

✗ Unbillable admin time (proposals, invoicing, chasing payments)  
✗ Personal tasks or non-client work  
✗ Excessive detail (e.g. "10:34am - 10:47am: replied to email" — just write "Email correspondence: 0.25 hours")  

Keep it clean. The client wants to see what you worked on and verify the hours match what they remember. They do not need a forensic activity log.

## How to calculate GBP from hours — decimal format and rounding

Most UK freelancers use **decimal hours** (1.5, 2.25, 3.75) not clock time (1:30, 2:15, 3:45) because it makes invoicing calculations easier.

### Decimal hour conversion

| Minutes worked | Decimal hours |
| --- | --- |
| 15 minutes | 0.25 |
| 30 minutes | 0.5 |
| 45 minutes | 0.75 |
| 1 hour 15 minutes | 1.25 |
| 1 hour 30 minutes | 1.5 |
| 2 hours 15 minutes | 2.25 |

### Calculation example (hourly rate)

**Rate:** £50/hour  
**Hours worked this week:** 14.5 hours  

**Calculation:**  
14.5 × £50 = **£725.00**

### Calculation example (day rate)

**Rate:** £600/day  
**Days worked:** 3.5 days  

**Calculation:**  
3.5 × £600 = **£2,100.00**

**Tip:** Round to the nearest 0.25 hours (15 minutes) unless you track to the exact minute. A 23-minute task becomes 0.25 hours. A 38-minute task becomes 0.5 hours. Clients understand 15-minute increments. Billing 0.38 hours looks pedantic.

## Billable vs unbillable hours — what to show on a client timesheet

A client-facing timesheet only shows **billable hours** — time spent on their project that you can charge for.

### Billable hours (show on timesheet)

✓ Client work — Design, code, writing, strategy, research  
✓ Client meetings — Discovery calls, feedback sessions, presentations  
✓ Revisions — Changes requested within agreed scope  
✓ Project-related admin — File prep, exporting deliverables, uploading to client's system  

### Unbillable hours (do NOT show on client timesheet)

✗ Proposals and pitching — Time spent winning the work  
✗ Invoicing and accounting — Admin for your business  
✗ Chasing late payments — Not the client's problem (though it is caused by them!)  
✗ Learning new tools — Personal development  
✗ Marketing and networking — Business development  

**Example:** You worked 18 hours this week. 14 hours were client work (billable). 4 hours were proposal writing and invoicing (unbillable). Your timesheet shows 14 hours. You track the other 4 hours privately to understand your true hourly rate.

**Why this matters:** If you bill £50/hour for 14 billable hours, you earn £700. But you actually worked 18 hours total, so your real hourly rate is £700 ÷ 18 = £38.89/hour. Track unbillable time so you know if you need to raise your rates.

## When to use a timesheet vs fixed-price — the decision guide

Choosing between time-based and fixed-price affects whether you need a timesheet. Here is how to decide.

### Use **hourly or day-rate (with timesheet)** when:

✓ **The scope is unclear** — Client does not know exactly what they want yet  
✓ **The project is exploratory** — Strategy, research, or discovery phases  
✓ **The client wants flexibility** — Ability to change direction without renegotiating a fixed price  
✓ **You are working on retainer** — Monthly hour bank for ongoing support  
✓ **You want predictable income** — Every hour worked = guaranteed payment  

**Risk:** If you are slow or inefficient, you still get paid. But the client might feel they are overpaying. If you are very fast, you earn less per project than you could on fixed-price.

### Use **fixed-price (no timesheet)** when:

✓ **The scope is crystal clear** — Both sides know exactly what will be delivered  
✓ **You have done this type of project many times** — You can estimate accurately  
✓ **The client prefers certainty** — They want a guaranteed price before committing  
✓ **You want to reward efficiency** — If you deliver fast, you earn more per hour  

**Risk:** If the scope expands, you are working for free unless you renegotiate. Always use a [scope of work](/blog/freelance-scope-of-work-template) to define boundaries.

**Bottom line:** Hourly/day-rate protects you when scope is uncertain. Fixed-price protects the client when scope is locked. Choose based on project clarity and risk.

## What NOT to put on a client-facing timesheet

Timesheets are professional documents. Do not include:

✗ **Excessive detail** — "10:42am - 10:58am: Replied to your email asking about logo colours." Just write "Email correspondence: 0.25 hours."  
✗ **Unbillable admin** — Proposals, invoicing, chasing payments. The client does not pay for your business admin.  
✗ **Personal tasks** — "Lunch break," "dentist appointment," "coffee with another client." Only show work for this client.  
✗ **Rounded-up padding** — If a task took 20 minutes, do not call it 1 hour. Round fairly to 0.25 or 0.5 hours.  
✗ **Complaints or editorial comments** — "Spent 2 hours fixing mistakes in the brief you sent" becomes "Brief clarification and requirements review: 2.0 hours."  

Keep it factual. The timesheet is evidence, not a diary.

## How to handle client disputes over timesheet hours

Sometimes a client will question the hours on your timesheet. Stay calm and follow this process.

### Step 1: Ask which specific hours they are disputing

> "Thanks for raising this. Can you let me know which tasks or dates you are concerned about? I am happy to provide more detail."

Do not get defensive. The client might just need more context.

### Step 2: Provide supporting evidence

If they dispute a 3-hour task, show:

✓ Meeting confirmations (calendar invite or email)  
✓ Slack or email timestamps proving when work was done  
✓ Deliverable files with creation/modified dates  
✓ Screenshots of the work in progress  

**Example:**

> "The 3 hours on 25 Sept were for revisions following your feedback email sent at 10:14am. I made the changes and sent the updated files at 2:47pm. Happy to share the email thread if that helps."

### Step 3: Refer to your contract or rate agreement

If the client says "I did not agree to this many hours," check your contract:

✓ **Hourly agreement:** "As per our contract, I bill for all hours worked on your project. The timesheet shows the work completed."  
✓ **Retainer:** "Your retainer includes 20 hours/month. I used 18.5 hours this month, which is within the agreed allowance."  

If your contract does not protect you, this is a lesson for next time. Tighten your scope and approval process.

### Step 4: Offer to adjust (only if you genuinely made a mistake)

If you overbilled by accident, own it:

> "You are right — I miscalculated the hours for that task. The correct total is 2.5 hours, not 3.5. I will issue a revised invoice."

But if the hours are accurate and the client is just trying to reduce the bill, stand firm:

> "I understand the hours are higher than expected. The timesheet is accurate and reflects the work completed as per your brief. If you would like to discuss a different billing structure for future work, I am happy to explore that."

Do not apologise for billing accurately. If the client refuses to pay a legitimate invoice, see [how to chase a late invoice](/blog/how-to-chase-a-late-invoice) for your UK statutory rights.

## Internal vs client-facing timesheets — what is the difference?

You should track time in two ways:

1. **Internal timesheet (for you):** Tracks ALL hours — billable, unbillable, admin, learning, proposals  
2. **Client timesheet (for them):** Only shows billable hours on their project

| **Billable hours** | | **10.5** | |

This internal view helps you understand:

✓ How much time you spend on non-billable work  
✓ Whether you need to raise your rates to cover admin time  
✓ Which clients take up the most time  

### Client timesheet example (send to client)

| Date | Task | Hours |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 23 Sept | Brand research | 3.5 |
| 24 Sept | Logo concepts | 5.0 |
| 25 Sept | Client feedback | 2.0 |

The client only sees their billable hours. They do not need to know you spent 1.5 hours writing a proposal for another client or 1 hour invoicing.

## VAT on timesheets and invoices

Timesheets do not show VAT — they only show hours and rates. VAT is added on the **invoice**.

### Timesheet (no VAT)

| Task | Hours | Rate (£/hr) | Total (£) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Design work | 12.0 | 50 | 600.00 |

### Invoice (VAT added)

| Description | Amount (£) |
| --- | --- |
| Design services — 12.0 hours @ £50/hour | 600.00 |
| **Net total** | **£600.00** |
| VAT @ 20% | **£120.00** |
| **Gross total due** | **£720.00** |

If you are **not VAT registered**, do not charge VAT. Your invoice shows:

> "Total: £600.00 (VAT not applicable)"

**VAT threshold:** You must register for VAT if your taxable turnover exceeds **£90,000** in a rolling 12-month period (as of 1 September 2026).

**Source:** [GOV.UK — Register for VAT](https://www.gov.uk/register-for-vat)

For more on UK freelance VAT, see [when to register for VAT UK freelancers](/blog/when-to-register-for-vat-uk-freelancers).

### Option 1: Spreadsheet (Google Sheets or Excel)

**Pros:**  
✓ Free  
✓ Fully customisable  
✓ Works offline (Excel) or online (Google Sheets)

✗ Manual data entry (you must remember to log hours)

**Best for:** Freelancers with simple projects, low client volume, or tight budgets.

## Common timesheet mistakes that lose you money

**Forgetting to log hours immediately.** You finish a task and think "I will log that later." You forget. You undercharge. Log hours as you go or set a daily reminder.

**Rounding down too much.** A 23-minute task is 0.25 hours, not 0. A 38-minute task is 0.5 hours, not 0.25. Do not undervalue your time.

**Not tracking unbillable hours.** You think you earn £50/hour because that is what clients pay. But if you work 30 billable hours and 10 unbillable hours per week, your real rate is £1,500 ÷ 40 = £37.50/hour. Track everything to know if you need to raise your rates.

**Vague task descriptions.** "Design work: 5 hours" tells the client nothing. Write "Logo design — 3 concept directions: 5 hours." Clear descriptions prevent disputes.

**Not agreeing on timesheet format upfront.** Some clients want detailed task breakdowns. Others just want total hours. Ask before you send your first timesheet so you do not have to redo it.

**Sending timesheets late.** If you bill weekly, send the timesheet and invoice every Friday. If you bill monthly, send it on the last working day of the month. Late timesheets delay payment.
