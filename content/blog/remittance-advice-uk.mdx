## What remittance advice is (and what it is not)

**Remittance advice** (also called a **remittance notice** or **payment advice**) is a document your client sends to tell you:

✓ They have sent (or will send) a payment  
✓ Which invoices the payment covers  
✓ How much they paid  
✓ The payment date and payment reference  

It is a **notification**, not a receipt. Think of it like a shipping note: "We have sent this. It should arrive soon."

### Example remittance advice content

```
From: Northline Studio Ltd — Accounts Payable
To: Oak & Pixel
Payment date: 8 October 2026
Payment method: BACS
Payment reference: NS-OCT26

Invoice no.   Invoice date   Amount       Payment
INV-2026-078  10 Sept 2026   £1,200.00    £1,200.00
INV-2026-081  20 Sept 2026   £2,400.00    £2,400.00

Total paid: £3,600.00
```

This tells Oak & Pixel that Northline has paid two invoices totalling £3,600, and the payment should arrive with reference "NS-OCT26".

### What remittance advice is NOT

**It is not an invoice** — An invoice is your demand for payment. Remittance advice is the client's notification that they have paid (or will pay).

**It is not a receipt** — A receipt confirms money has changed hands and is in your account. Remittance advice says the payment has been instructed, not that it has cleared.

**It is not proof of payment** — Proof of payment is your bank statement showing the money has landed. Always check your bank, even if you receive remittance advice.

**It is not a statement of account** — A statement of account is a summary you send to the client showing all invoices and payments over a period. Remittance advice is sent by the client to you about a specific payment.

**Bottom line:** Remittance advice is a payment notification. It tells you what to expect in your bank. It does not replace checking your bank statement.

## Why clients send remittance advice (and why it helps you)

### Why clients send it

Corporate clients and larger businesses send remittance advice automatically when they process payments. It is part of their accounts payable workflow.

**Common reasons:**

✓ Their accounting software generates it when they approve a batch of payments  
✓ Their finance team needs an audit trail showing which invoices were paid  
✓ They pay multiple suppliers at once and need to tell each one which invoices the payment covers  
✓ They want to avoid you chasing for payment when the transfer takes a few days to clear  

Public sector clients (councils, NHS, government departments) almost always send remittance advice. It is required for their audit and transparency processes.

### Why it helps you

Remittance advice makes payment reconciliation faster and clearer.

**Without remittance advice:**  
A payment of £3,600 arrives in your bank. You have five open invoices. Which ones were paid? You email the client's accounts team and wait for a reply. It takes days to figure it out.

**With remittance advice:**  
The remittance advice lists the two invoices covered (INV-2026-078 and INV-2026-081). You match them immediately, mark them paid, and move on. No guessing. No delay.

**Bottom line:** Remittance advice saves you time and confusion when reconciling payments. If your client does not send it, you can ask them to start.

## How to read remittance advice line by line

Remittance advice layouts vary by company, but most include the same core information. Here is what to look for.

### Field 1: Sender details (who is paying you)

**What to check:**  
The company name and accounts payable contact details.

**Why it matters:**  
Confirm the remittance advice is from the client you invoiced. If the company name does not match, query it before assuming the payment is yours.

**Example:**  
```
From: Lumen Digital Ltd — Finance Department
Email: accounts@lumendigital.co.uk
```

### Field 2: Recipient details (you)

**What to check:**  
Your business name and address.

**Why it matters:**  
If the recipient name is wrong, the payment might have gone to the wrong supplier. Contact the client immediately to clarify.

**Example:**  
```
To: Northline Studio
14 Canal Street, Manchester, M1 3EZ
```

### Field 3: Payment date

**What to check:**  
The date the client sent (or will send) the payment.

**Why it matters:**  
BACS payments take 1-3 working days. Faster Payments are usually same-day. If the payment date was three days ago and nothing has arrived, chase it.

**Example:**  
```
Payment date: 8 October 2026
```

### Field 4: Payment method

**What to check:**  
BACS, Faster Payment, CHAPS, cheque, or bank transfer.

**Why it matters:**  
This tells you when to expect the money. BACS takes 3 working days. Faster Payments arrive within hours.

**Example:**  
```
Payment method: BACS
```

### Field 5: Payment reference

**What to check:**  
The reference code the client used when making the bank transfer.

**Why it matters:**  
When the payment arrives in your bank, the reference will match this. Use it to confirm the right payment has landed.

**Example:**  
```
Payment reference: NS-OCT26
```

If your bank shows a payment with reference "NS-OCT26", you know it matches this remittance advice.

### Field 6: Invoice list

**What to check:**  
A table listing the invoice numbers, dates, and amounts the payment covers.

**Why it matters:**  
This is the most important section. Match each invoice to your records and check the amounts.

**Example:**  
```
Invoice no.   Invoice date   Amount       Payment
INV-2026-078  10 Sept 2026   £1,200.00    £1,200.00
INV-2026-081  20 Sept 2026   £2,400.00    £2,400.00
```

**What to do:**

1. Find each invoice in your records (your invoice log, accounting software, or files)
2. Check the invoice date matches
3. Check the amount matches
4. Check the "Payment" column shows the full amount (or note if it is a partial payment)

If the invoice number is wrong, or the amount does not match, stop and query it with the client before marking anything as paid.

### Field 7: Total payment amount

**What to check:**  
The total of all invoices being paid.

**Why it matters:**  
When the payment arrives in your bank, the amount should match this total (or be close, allowing for bank charges if international).

**Example:**  
```
Total paid: £3,600.00
```

### Field 8: Notes or deductions (if any)

**What to check:**  
Any notes explaining deductions, adjustments, credits, or withheld amounts.

**Why it matters:**  
If the payment is less than the invoice total, the notes should explain why. Common reasons: early payment discount taken, expenses reimbursed separately, tax withheld (rare in UK), or dispute over scope.

**Example:**  
```
Notes: £100 deducted for client-provided stock images
```

If there are deductions and you did not agree to them, query the payment immediately. Do not accept short payments without explanation.

### Remittance advice checklist

Use this every time you receive remittance advice:

✓ Sender is the client you invoiced  
✓ Recipient name (your business) is correct  
✓ Payment date is recent (if old, chase it)  
✓ Payment method tells you when to expect the money (BACS = 3 days, Faster Payment = same day)  
✓ Payment reference matches what you will see in your bank  
✓ Every invoice listed matches your records (number, date, amount)  
✓ Total payment amount is correct  
✓ Check your bank within the expected timeframe  
✓ If amounts do not match, query immediately  

## What to do when remittance advice does not match your invoices

Sometimes the remittance advice shows a different amount, pays the wrong invoice, or lists invoices you do not recognise. Here is what to do.

### Scenario 1: Short payment (they paid less than the invoice)

**What it looks like:**

```
Invoice no.   Amount       Payment
INV-2026-078  £1,200.00    £1,100.00
```

Your invoice was £1,200. The remittance advice shows £1,100 paid.

**What to do:**

1. Check the remittance advice for notes explaining the deduction
2. If no explanation, email the client's accounts team immediately
3. Do not accept short payment without written explanation
4. If it is a genuine error, ask them to send the remaining £100
5. If they claim a discount you did not agree to, refer to your contract and chase the full amount

**Copy-paste email — querying short payment:**

**Subject:** Query on payment — Invoice INV-2026-078

Hi [Accounts team name],

Thank you for the remittance advice for invoice INV-2026-078.

The remittance advice shows £1,100 paid, but the invoice was for £1,200. I cannot see a note explaining the £100 difference.

Can you confirm why the payment was short? If this was an error, please send the remaining £100. If there was a reason for the deduction, can you send me written details so I can reconcile my records?

Thanks,  
[Your name]

### Scenario 2: One payment covering multiple invoices (but one invoice is missing)

**What it looks like:**

You have three open invoices (INV-2026-078 for £1,200, INV-2026-081 for £2,400, and INV-2026-085 for £1,800). The remittance advice lists only two:

```
Invoice no.   Amount       Payment
INV-2026-078  £1,200.00    £1,200.00
INV-2026-081  £2,400.00    £2,400.00

Total paid: £3,600.00
```

Invoice INV-2026-085 (£1,800) is not included.

**What to do:**

1. Mark the two invoices listed as paid
2. Email accounts to query the missing invoice
3. Ask when they will pay it

**Copy-paste email — querying missing invoice:**

**Subject:** Query on remittance advice — Invoice INV-2026-085 missing

Hi [Accounts team name],

Thank you for the remittance advice and payment of £3,600 covering invoices INV-2026-078 and INV-2026-081.

I notice invoice INV-2026-085 (£1,800, due 11 September) was not included in this payment. Can you confirm when that invoice will be paid?

Let me know if you need me to resend the invoice or if there is anything blocking payment.

Thanks,  
[Your name]

### Scenario 3: Wrong invoice number listed

**What it looks like:**

The remittance advice lists "INV-2026-088" but you never sent that invoice. You sent INV-2026-078.

**What to do:**

1. Check your records to confirm the invoice number is wrong
2. Email accounts with the correct invoice number
3. Ask them to send a corrected remittance advice

**Copy-paste email — correcting invoice number:**

**Subject:** Correction needed — Remittance advice invoice number

Hi [Accounts team name],

Thank you for the remittance advice and payment.

The remittance advice references invoice **INV-2026-088**, but the correct invoice number is **INV-2026-078** (dated 10 September, £1,200).

Can you confirm the payment was for INV-2026-078 and send a corrected remittance advice for your records?

Thanks,  
[Your name]

### Scenario 4: Payment amount arrived in bank but does not match remittance advice total

**What it looks like:**

Remittance advice says total paid is £3,600. Your bank shows £3,580 arrived.

**What to do:**

1. Check if the difference is bank charges (common for international payments, rare for UK BACS)
2. If UK payment, the amount should match exactly — query it
3. If international, check your contract to see who pays bank fees

**Copy-paste email — querying bank amount mismatch:**

**Subject:** Query on payment amount — Bank vs remittance advice

Hi [Accounts team name],

Thank you for the remittance advice showing £3,600 total payment.

The amount that arrived in my bank is £3,580 — a difference of £20. Can you confirm why the amounts do not match?

If this was a bank charge deduction, can you send me written confirmation so I can reconcile my records correctly?

Thanks,  
[Your name]

### Scenario 5: Remittance advice received, but payment never arrives

**What it looks like:**

The remittance advice is dated 8 October. It is now 15 October (seven days later, five working days). No payment has appeared in your bank.

**What to do:**

1. Wait until at least three working days have passed (BACS clearing time)
2. Check your bank carefully (look for the payment reference from the remittance advice)
3. If nothing has arrived after five working days, email accounts to chase it

**Copy-paste email — chasing missing payment:**

**Subject:** Chasing payment — Remittance advice dated 8 October

Hi [Accounts team name],

I received remittance advice on 8 October showing a payment of £3,600 (reference NS-OCT26) covering invoices INV-2026-078 and INV-2026-081.

It has now been five working days and the payment has not arrived in my bank. Can you check whether the payment was sent and confirm the payment details (amount, reference, and bank account used)?

If the payment was not sent, can you arrange it urgently? The invoices were due over two weeks ago.

Thanks,  
[Your name]

## How to ask a client to send remittance advice (if they do not normally)

Many smaller companies and sole traders do not send remittance advice automatically. If your client does not send it, you can ask them to start.

### Why you should ask

Remittance advice makes reconciliation faster. Without it, you have to guess which invoices a payment covers. With it, you know instantly.

It is especially useful when:

✓ The client pays multiple invoices at once  
✓ They pay from a different company name than the one you invoiced  
✓ You have several open invoices and need to know which were paid  
✓ Payments take a few days to clear (BACS) and you want advance notice  

### Copy-paste email — requesting remittance advice

**Subject:** Request for remittance advice with payments

Hi [Accounts team name],

To help me reconcile payments more quickly, would you be able to send remittance advice whenever you make a payment?

Remittance advice is a short note or document that lists:

• The invoice number(s) being paid  
• The payment amount  
• The payment date and reference  

This helps me match payments to invoices and reduces the need for follow-up queries. Many of my clients send this automatically from their accounting software.

Let me know if this is possible, and thank you for considering it.

Best,  
[Your name]

Most clients will agree. It is a standard practice in accounts payable and takes minimal effort on their side.

## Fake remittance advice phishing — how to spot it and what to do

Scammers send fake "remittance advice" emails to trick you into clicking malicious links or opening attachments. They impersonate clients, banks, or payment processors.

### How the scam works

You receive an email:

```
Subject: Payment remittance advice — Urgent action required
From: accounts@lumendigitaI.co.uk  [fake domain — note the capital I instead of lowercase L]

Dear supplier,

We have processed payment for invoice INV-2026-078. Please click the link below to view your remittance advice and confirm receipt:

[View Remittance Advice]

If you do not confirm within 24 hours, the payment may be cancelled.

Lumen Digital Finance Team
```

**Red flags:**

✗ Sender email domain is fake (lumendigitaI.co.uk instead of lumendigital.co.uk — capital I vs lowercase L)  
✗ Asks you to click a link to "view" remittance advice (real remittance advice is attached as PDF or in the email body)  
✗ Urgent wording ("within 24 hours", "payment may be cancelled")  
✗ Generic greeting ("Dear supplier" instead of your business name)  
✗ Spelling or grammar errors  
✗ Unexpected attachment with a suspicious file name (e.g. "remittance_advice.exe" or "payment.zip")  

### What the scam tries to do

**If you click the link:**  
It takes you to a fake login page designed to steal your banking credentials, accounting software password, or personal details.

**If you open the attachment:**  
It installs malware on your computer to steal files, passwords, or banking information.

### How to spot fake remittance advice

✓ **Check the sender email domain carefully** — Scammers use lookalike domains (lumendigitaI.co.uk vs lumendigital.co.uk, lumen-digital.co.uk, lumendigital.net). Hover over the sender address to see the real domain.  
✓ **Real remittance advice is attached as PDF or in the email body** — If you need to "click to view" it, it is a scam.  
✓ **No legitimate payment notification uses urgent deadlines** — Real accounts teams do not threaten to cancel payments if you do not confirm within 24 hours.  
✓ **Check for spelling and grammar errors** — Professional finance teams proofread their emails.  
✓ **Be suspicious of unexpected remittance advice** — If you did not invoice the sender recently, it might be a scam.  

### What to do if you receive a suspicious remittance advice email

**Do NOT:**

✗ Click any links in the email  
✗ Open any attachments  
✗ Reply to the email  
✗ Enter your details on any linked page  

**DO:**

✓ **Check your bank directly** — Log in to your bank through the official website (not via a link in the email). See if a payment has actually arrived.  
✓ **Contact the client through a known method** — Phone them or email the accounts team using the email address you already have on file (not the one in the suspicious email).  
✓ **Report the email to the NCSC** — Forward the suspicious email to **report@phishing.gov.uk** (the National Cyber Security Centre's Suspicious Email Reporting Service). This is the official UK reporting address.  
✓ **Report to Action Fraud** — If you clicked a link, opened an attachment, or entered details, report it to **Action Fraud** at [www.actionfraud.police.uk](https://www.actionfraud.police.uk) or call 0300 123 2040. Action Fraud is the UK's national fraud and cybercrime reporting centre.  
✓ **Delete the email** — Once reported, delete it from your inbox and trash.  

### Official reporting routes (verified October 2026)

**National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) — Suspicious Email Reporting Service:**  
Email: **report@phishing.gov.uk**  
Website: [www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/phishing-scams)

**Action Fraud — National fraud and cybercrime reporting:**  
Website: [www.actionfraud.police.uk](https://www.actionfraud.police.uk)  
Phone: **0300 123 2040** (24/7)

If you entered banking details or transferred money to a scammer, contact your bank immediately. They may be able to stop the transfer or recover the funds.

### Bottom line on fake remittance advice

✓ Real remittance advice comes as a PDF attachment or plain email text — never a "click to view" link  
✓ Sender email domain must match your client's real domain exactly  
✓ Legitimate payment notifications do not use urgent deadlines or threaten cancellation  
✓ When in doubt, check your bank directly and contact the client through known channels  
✓ Report suspicious emails to **report@phishing.gov.uk** and Action Fraud

## If you pay subcontractors: a short remittance advice template to send

If you hire subcontractors or freelancers and pay their invoices, you can send remittance advice to help them reconcile payments.

### Why you should send it

Sending remittance advice is good practice. It tells your subcontractor:

✓ You have paid them (or will pay them soon)  
✓ Which invoices the payment covers  
✓ When to expect the money in their bank  

It saves them chasing you and makes you look professional.

### Copy-paste remittance advice template

**Subject:** Payment remittance advice — Invoice [invoice number]

Hi [Subcontractor name],

This is to confirm we have processed payment for the following invoice(s):

**Invoice number:** INV-SUB-042  
**Invoice date:** 15 September 2026  
**Amount:** £800.00  

**Payment details:**  
Payment date: 8 October 2026  
Payment method: BACS  
Payment reference: OAKPIXEL-OCT  
Amount paid: £800.00  

The payment should arrive in your bank within 1-3 working days.

Let me know if you have any questions.

Best,  
[Your name]  
[Your business name]

If you pay multiple invoices at once, list them in a table:

```
Invoice no.       Invoice date   Amount       Payment
INV-SUB-042       15 Sept 2026   £800.00      £800.00
INV-SUB-045       22 Sept 2026   £1,200.00    £1,200.00

Total paid: £2,000.00
```

### Formal remittance advice format (optional)

If you want a more formal document, save this as a PDF and attach it to the email:

```
REMITTANCE ADVICE

From:
Oak & Pixel Ltd
28 Park Lane, Bristol, BS2 8QR
billing@oakpixel.co.uk

To:
[Subcontractor business name]
[Subcontractor address]

Date: 8 October 2026

Payment details:
Payment date: 8 October 2026
Payment method: BACS
Payment reference: OAKPIXEL-OCT

Invoice(s) paid:

Invoice no.       Invoice date   Amount
INV-SUB-042       15 Sept 2026   £800.00

Total amount paid: £800.00

Notes: Payment sent via BACS. Expected to clear within 1-3 working days.
```

Most subcontractors do not need formal PDF remittance advice. A clear email is enough. Use the PDF format if your client or accountant requires formal documentation for audit.

## Worked example: a designer reconciling remittance advice for three invoices

Here is a step-by-step example showing how a UK freelance designer handles remittance advice from a client.

### Scenario

**Freelancer:** Northline Studio (not VAT registered)  
**Client:** Lumen Digital Ltd  
**Invoices sent:**
- INV-2026-078: £1,200 (logo design, due 24 September 2026)
- INV-2026-081: £2,400 (brand guidelines, due 4 October 2026)
- INV-2026-085: £1,600 (social templates, due 11 October 2026)

**Date:** 8 October 2026

### Step 1: Northline receives remittance advice email

The email from Lumen's finance team says:

```
Subject: Payment remittance advice — October 2026

Hi Northline Studio,

Please see attached remittance advice for our October payment.

Best,
Lumen Digital Finance Team
```

Attached PDF:

```
REMITTANCE ADVICE

From: Lumen Digital Ltd — Finance Department
To: Northline Studio, 14 Canal Street, Manchester, M1 3EZ

Payment date: 8 October 2026
Payment method: BACS
Payment reference: LUMEN-OCT26

Invoice no.   Invoice date   Invoice amount   Payment
INV-2026-078  10 Sept 2026   £1,200.00        £1,200.00
INV-2026-081  20 Sept 2026   £2,400.00        £2,200.00

Total paid: £3,400.00

Notes: £200 deducted from INV-2026-081 for client-provided stock images as agreed.
```

### Step 2: Northline reads the remittance advice and spots a discrepancy

Northline checks the details:

✓ Sender is correct (Lumen Digital Ltd)  
✓ Recipient name is correct (Northline Studio)  
✓ Payment date is 8 October (today)  
✓ Payment method is BACS (expect money in 1-3 working days)  
✓ Payment reference is LUMEN-OCT26 (will match bank transfer)  
✓ INV-2026-078 is correct (£1,200 invoiced, £1,200 paid)  
✗ **INV-2026-081 shows £2,400 invoiced but only £2,200 paid**  
✗ **INV-2026-085 (£1,600) is not included**  

The notes explain the £200 deduction on INV-2026-081: "client-provided stock images as agreed."

Northline checks their records. They did agree the client would provide stock images, and they adjusted the project scope to deduct £200 for that. The deduction is correct.

But INV-2026-085 (£1,600) is missing from the payment.

### Step 3: Northline updates their invoice log

Northline marks INV-2026-078 and INV-2026-081 as "Paid (pending bank confirmation)" in their invoice log:

| Invoice no. | Amount | Status | Notes |
|-------------|--------|--------|-------|
| INV-2026-078 | £1,200 | Paid (pending) | Remittance advice received 8 Oct |
| INV-2026-081 | £2,400 | Paid £2,200 (pending) | £200 deducted for client stock images |
| INV-2026-085 | £1,600 | Unpaid | Not included in Oct payment |

### Step 4: Northline checks their bank

Three working days later (11 October), Northline checks their bank statement.

**Bank entry:**

```
Date: 11 Oct 2026
Description: BACS payment — LUMEN-OCT26
Amount: £3,400.00
```

The payment has arrived. The reference matches the remittance advice (LUMEN-OCT26). The amount matches the remittance advice total (£3,400).

Northline updates their invoice log:

| Invoice no. | Amount | Status | Notes |
|-------------|--------|--------|-------|
| INV-2026-078 | £1,200 | Paid ✓ | Bank cleared 11 Oct |
| INV-2026-081 | £2,400 | Paid £2,200 ✓ | £200 deducted, bank cleared 11 Oct |
| INV-2026-085 | £1,600 | Unpaid | Not included in Oct payment |

### Step 5: Northline queries the missing invoice

Northline emails Lumen's finance team to ask about INV-2026-085:

## Receiver checklist: what to do every time you receive remittance advice

Use this checklist every time a client sends remittance advice:

**1. Read the remittance advice carefully**

✓ Check sender is your client  
✓ Check recipient name (your business) is correct  
✓ Note the payment date and method (BACS = 3 days, Faster Payment = same day)  
✓ Note the payment reference (you will see this in your bank)  

**2. Match the invoices**

✓ List every invoice number on the remittance advice  
✓ Find each invoice in your records (invoice log, accounting software, or files)  
✓ Check invoice dates match  
✓ Check amounts match  

**3. Check for discrepancies**

✓ Short payment? Query it immediately (unless notes explain an agreed deduction)  
✓ Missing invoices? Email accounts to ask when they will be paid  
✓ Wrong invoice number? Email accounts to correct it  
✓ Unexpected deductions? Ask for written explanation  

**4. Wait for the payment to arrive in your bank**

✓ BACS payments take 1-3 working days  
✓ Faster Payments usually arrive same day or next day  
✓ Check the payment reference matches the remittance advice  
✓ Check the amount matches the remittance advice total  

**5. Mark invoices as paid only after the money has cleared**

✓ Remittance advice alone is not proof of payment  
✓ Check your bank statement  
✓ Once the payment has cleared, mark the invoices as paid in your records  

**6. If payment does not arrive**

✓ Wait at least three working days (BACS clearing time)  
✓ Check your bank carefully (search for the payment reference)  
✓ If nothing has arrived after five working days, email accounts to chase it  

**7. Keep the remittance advice on file**

✓ Save the PDF or email in the same folder as the invoices  
✓ If HMRC audits you, the remittance advice proves when the client paid  
✓ It also helps you reconcile if the client queries a payment months later

## FAQ

### Can a client refuse to send remittance advice?

Yes, but most will not. Remittance advice is standard practice in accounts payable. If a client refuses, they have no legal obligation to send it, but you can explain it saves both of you time on reconciliation queries. If they still refuse, track payments manually by matching bank references to invoices.

### Should I send a receipt after receiving remittance advice and payment?

Only if the client asks for one. Most clients do not need a receipt — their bank statement and your invoice are enough. If they request written confirmation that you received the payment, send a short email confirming the payment date, amount, and invoice numbers.

### What is the difference between remittance advice and a payment reference?

A payment reference is the short code or text the client enters when making the bank transfer (e.g. "INV-2026-078" or "LUMEN-OCT26"). Remittance advice is the full document listing all invoices, amounts, and payment details. The payment reference appears on both the remittance advice and your bank statement.

### Do I need remittance advice if the client always pays on time and in full?

No, but it helps. If the client pays one invoice at a time and always includes the invoice number in the payment reference, you can match payments without remittance advice. But if they pay multiple invoices at once, or use vague payment references, remittance advice saves you time.

### What if remittance advice shows a payment for an invoice I never sent?

Contact the client immediately. They may have paid the wrong supplier, or their accounts team may have used the wrong invoice number. Do not accept the payment until you have clarified which invoice it was meant for. If the money arrives in your bank and it was a mistake, offer to return it.

### Can remittance advice be sent after the payment arrives?

Yes, though it is less helpful. Ideally, remittance advice is sent when the payment is made (or a day before) so you know what to expect. If it arrives after the money has cleared, it still helps you reconcile which invoices were paid, but you lose the advance warning.
