## What a quote is (and when UK freelancers need one)

A **quote** is a fixed price offer for defined work. You agree to deliver a specific outcome for a specific amount. Once the client accepts, that price stands — unless scope changes.

**Example:** "5-page website with homepage, about, services, portfolio, contact. Includes 2 rounds of revisions. Total: £3,000. Valid until 31 October 2026."

A quote is binding. If the client says yes, you cannot later say "actually, it is £4,000." And if you quote £3,000, the client cannot later say "I will only pay £2,500" unless you agree to reduce scope.

### Quote vs estimate — what is the difference?

A **quote** is a fixed price. The client knows exactly what they will pay. You know exactly what you will deliver.

An **estimate** is a best guess. The final price can change based on time, materials, or discoveries made during the work.

**When to use a quote:**

✓ The scope is clear and unlikely to change  
✓ You have done similar projects before and can price accurately  
✓ The client needs a guaranteed price to get internal approval  
✓ The project is under £10,000 and straightforward  

**When to use an estimate:**

✓ The scope is uncertain or exploratory  
✓ You cannot know how long it will take until you start  
✓ The client just needs a ballpark figure for budgeting  
✓ The project involves unknowns (technical discovery, integrations, legacy code)  

**Bottom line:** Quote when scope is locked. Estimate when scope is uncertain. Most UK freelancers use quotes for brand design, web builds, and consulting projects with clear deliverables.

For larger or more complex projects, see [freelance proposal templates](/blog/one-page-freelance-proposal-template-uk) and [scope of work templates](/blog/freelance-scope-of-work-template).

## When to send a quote — the decision tree

Not every enquiry needs a formal quote. Here is when to send one and when to skip it.

### Send a quote when:

✓ The client asks "how much will this cost?"  
✓ The scope is clear enough to price (you know what you are delivering)  
✓ The project is over £500 and the client needs something in writing  
✓ The client is comparing you to other freelancers and needs a formal document  
✓ You need to define boundaries (what is included, what is not) to prevent scope creep  

### Skip the quote and just send a price when:

✓ The project is under £300 and the client trusts you (e.g. repeat customer)  
✓ The scope is tiny and obvious (e.g. "3 Instagram templates — £150")  
✓ You are quoting hourly or day-rate and the hours are not fixed (use a rate card instead)  

**Tip:** If the client says "just give me a rough idea," that is an estimate, not a quote. Reply: "Based on what you have described, I estimate £X,XXX–£X,XXX depending on final scope. I can provide a fixed quote once we clarify [specific unknowns]."

## What to include on a UK freelance quote — field checklist

A proper quote leaves no room for misunderstanding. Here is what to include.

### Required fields

✓ **Your business name and contact details** (address, email, phone)  
✓ **Client name and company** (if applicable)  
✓ **Quote number** (e.g. QUO-2026-012)  
✓ **Issue date**  
✓ **Valid-until date** (usually 30 days from issue)  
✓ **Project title or summary**  
✓ **Brief description of the outcome** (what problem you are solving)  
✓ **Itemised breakdown** (stage/deliverable, quantity or days, rate, line total)  
✓ **Subtotal**  
✓ **VAT line** (if you are VAT registered)  
✓ **Total in GBP**  
✓ **What is included** (scope, deliverables, revisions)  
✓ **What is not included** (out-of-scope items to prevent creep)  
✓ **Assumptions** (any conditions your price depends on)  
✓ **Timeline** (start date and delivery estimate)  
✓ **Deposit and payment terms** (e.g. 50% upfront, 50% on delivery)  
✓ **How to accept** ("Reply YES to confirm" or "Sign and return")  
✓ **What happens if scope changes** (additional work billed separately)  

### Helpful optional fields

✓ **Signature line** (for formal acceptance)  
✓ **Client address** (if needed for VAT or contracting)  
✓ **Purchase order reference** (if the client provided one)  
✓ **Your company registration number** (if limited company)  

**Do NOT include:**

✗ Vague deliverables ("website" — what kind? how many pages?)  
✗ Open-ended revisions ("unlimited changes")  
✗ No expiry date (old quotes can haunt you)

## Worked £ example for UK freelancers — checking the maths

Let's verify the numbers in the quote above.

**Day rate:** £500/day  
**Days:** 3 + 4 + 1 = 8 days  
**Subtotal:** 8 × £500 = £4,000  

**VAT registered?** Yes.  
**VAT rate:** 20% (standard rate in the UK)  
**VAT amount:** £4,000 × 0.20 = £800  

**Total:** £4,000 + £800 = £4,800  

**Deposit (50%):** £4,800 × 0.50 = £2,400  
**Balance (50%):** £4,800 × 0.50 = £2,400  

**Check:** £2,400 + £2,400 = £4,800 ✓

### If you are NOT VAT registered

Remove the VAT line entirely:

| **Subtotal** | | | **£4,000** |
| VAT | | | Not applicable (not VAT registered) |
| **Total** | | | **£4,000** |

**Deposit (50%):** £2,000  
**Balance (50%):** £2,000  

You must **not** charge VAT if you are not registered. Charging VAT when you are not registered is illegal.

**VAT registration threshold:** £90,000 taxable turnover in a rolling 12-month period (as of 1 September 2026).

## Quote vs estimate wording — what to say so the client understands

The words you use matter. If you say "quote" but the client thinks "estimate," you will have problems when the price changes.

### Fixed-price quote wording

Use this when scope is locked and the price will not change:

> "This is a **fixed-price quote** for the work described above. The price of £4,800 will not change unless you request additional work beyond the agreed scope."

### Ballpark estimate wording

Use this when scope is uncertain and the price might change:

> "This is an **estimate** based on the information provided. The final cost may vary depending on time required, scope changes, or unforeseen technical complexity. I will notify you if costs are likely to exceed this estimate and get your approval before proceeding."

### Hybrid wording (quote with conditions)

Use this when most scope is fixed but some parts depend on client input:

> "This quote is valid provided the assumptions listed above hold. If scope changes (e.g. additional pages, extra revisions, or features not listed), the price will be adjusted and you will be notified before any additional work begins."

**Tip:** Once a client accepts a quote, it can form a binding agreement. Make sure your included/excluded/assumptions sections are clear. If something changes, document it in writing and issue a new quote or change order.

## UK-specific notes for freelancers

### Validity period (30 days is standard)

Most UK freelancers use a 30-day validity period. This protects you from old quotes resurfacing months later when your rates or availability have changed.

**Example wording:**

> "This quote is valid until 4 November 2026. After that date, prices and availability may change."

If the client does not respond by the expiry date, follow up once. If they still do not commit, let the quote expire and reprice if they come back later.

### Consumer contracts (B2C freelancers)

If you are quoting a **consumer** (e.g. a private individual, not a business), you need to be aware of the **Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013**.

For contracts made **at a distance** (email, phone, website) or **off-premises** (at the consumer's home, workplace, or another location), consumers have a **14-day cancellation right** from the date they accept.

**What this means for quotes:**

✓ If you quote a private homeowner for graphic design work and they accept by email, they have 14 days to cancel without reason  
✓ You can ask them to waive the cancellation right if they want work to start immediately (get this in writing)  
✓ If you complete the work within the 14-day period and they then cancel, you may still be entitled to payment for work done — but disputes can arise  

**How to handle this:**

Include a note in your quote for consumer clients:

> "If you are a consumer (not acting in a business capacity), you have a legal right to cancel this contract within 14 days of acceptance. If you would like work to start immediately and waive this right, please confirm in writing. Once work begins, cancellation may still be possible but payment for completed work will be due."

For **business-to-business (B2B)** quotes, this does not apply. Most UK freelancers work B2B, so consumer contract rules are less common — but if you work with private individuals, be aware of them.

**Source:** [GOV.UK — Consumer Contracts Regulations](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/consumer-contracts-information-cancellation-and-additional-charges-regulations-the-consumer-contracts-regulations)

### VAT — registered vs not registered

If you are **VAT registered:**

✓ Add VAT at the standard rate (20%) unless the service qualifies for a reduced or zero rate (rare for freelance design, development, or consulting)  
✓ Show VAT separately: Subtotal £4,000, VAT £800, Total £4,800  
✓ State your VAT number on the quote  

If you are **not VAT registered:**

✓ Do not add VAT  
✓ Write "VAT not applicable" or "Not VAT registered" if the client asks  
✓ You cannot charge VAT if you are not registered — doing so is illegal  

**When to register:**

You **must** register for VAT if your **taxable turnover** exceeds £90,000 in a rolling 12-month period (as of 1 September 2026).

You **can** register voluntarily if your turnover is below that (useful if you pay VAT on expenses and want to reclaim it).

**Source:** [GOV.UK — VAT registration thresholds](https://www.gov.uk/vat-registration-thresholds)

For a full guide, see [when to register for VAT UK freelancers](/blog/when-to-register-for-vat-uk-freelancers).

## Email scripts — sending a quote and handling pushback

### Script 1 — Sending the quote (new client)

**Subject:** Quote for [Project name] — [Your business name]

> Hi [Client name],
>
> Thank you for the project brief. Attached is a detailed quote for [project name].
>
> **Summary:**  
> - 5-page website (homepage, about, services, portfolio, contact)  
> - Custom design and Webflow build with CRM integration  
> - Total: £4,800 (inc VAT)  
> - Deposit: £2,400 (50%) to start  
> - Timeline: 3–4 weeks from deposit and materials received  
>
> The quote is valid until [date — 30 days from now].
>
> If you have any questions or need adjustments, let me know. Otherwise, reply **"I accept the quote"** and I will send a deposit invoice and contract.
>
> Looking forward to working with you.
>
> Best,  
> [Your name]

### Script 2 — Client asks "can you do it cheaper?"

**Subject:** Re: Quote for [Project name]

> Hi [Client name],
>
> Thanks for your reply. I understand budget is a consideration.
>
> The quoted price reflects the time required to deliver the work at the quality you need. If the total is over budget, I can offer two options:
>
> 1. **Reduce scope** — We could drop one or two pages, reduce revision rounds, or remove the CRM integration. That would bring the price down to £X,XXX.  
> 2. **Spread payment over milestones** — Instead of 50/50, we could do 40% upfront, 30% mid-point, 30% on delivery. The total stays the same but the initial outlay is lower.  
>
> Let me know what works best for you, and I can revise the quote.
>
> Best,  
> [Your name]

**Tip:** Protect your day rate. Reduce the work, not your rate. If a client wants you to halve your price for the same work, walk away — that client will cause problems later.

### Script 3 — Client accepts verbally but does not pay the deposit

**Subject:** Re: Quote for [Project name]

> Hi [Client name],
>
> Thanks for confirming you are happy with the quote.
>
> Attached is the deposit invoice (£2,400 inc VAT). Once this clears, I will schedule the project start date and send the contract.
>
> Payment details are on the invoice. Please reference the invoice number in your bank transfer.
>
> Let me know if you need any other information to process payment, and I will get it sorted.
>
> Best,  
> [Your name]

**Never start work until the deposit has cleared.** "I sent it this morning" is not the same as "It is in your account."

For deposit invoice templates, see [deposit invoice template UK](/blog/deposit-invoice-template-uk).

### Script 4 — Client wants to negotiate after the quote is accepted

**Subject:** Re: Quote for [Project name]

> Hi [Client name],
>
> Thanks for your message. I understand you are now asking for [extra feature / additional pages / more revisions].
>
> That is a scope change beyond what was included in the original quote. I am happy to add it, but it will require a revised quote for the additional work.
>
> The original quote covered [deliverables]. Adding [new request] would take an extra [X days / hours], bringing the total to £X,XXX.
>
> Let me know if you would like me to send a revised quote, or if you would prefer to proceed with the original scope and add this later as a separate project.
>
> Best,  
> [Your name]

**Do not give away scope for free.** If the client tries to sneak in extra work, call it out immediately and reprice.

## Common mistakes that turn quotes into disputes

**No expiry date.** A client accepts a quote you sent 6 months ago. Your rates have gone up. You cannot honour the old price. Always include a valid-until date.

**Vague deliverables.** "Website" could mean 3 pages or 30 pages. Be specific: "5 pages: homepage, about, services, portfolio, contact."

**No included/excluded sections.** The client assumes copywriting, photography, and e-commerce are included. You assumed they were not. Add an exclusions list to every quote.

**Unlimited revisions.** You say "revisions included." The client asks for 12 rounds. Define how many: "2 rounds of design revisions, 1 round of build revisions."

**No deposit.** The client accepts the quote, you start work, they disappear. Always require a deposit (30–50%) before work begins.

**No scope change clause.** The client adds new pages, features, or deliverables and expects the price to stay the same. State clearly: "Additional work beyond this scope will be quoted separately."

**Charging VAT when not registered.** Illegal. Only charge VAT if you are VAT registered.

**Starting work before the quote is accepted.** The client says "yeah, sounds good" on a call. That is not acceptance. Get it in writing: "I accept the quote" or a signed document.

## Checklist before you send a quote

Use this checklist to make sure your quote is watertight.

✓ Quote number, issue date, and valid-until date included  
✓ Client name and project title stated  
✓ Deliverables listed specifically (not vague)  
✓ Itemised pricing breakdown with day rate or hourly rate shown  
✓ VAT added only if you are VAT registered  
✓ "What is included" section covers all deliverables  
✓ "What is not included" section states exclusions  
✓ Assumptions section covers dependencies (e.g. client provides copy, images, API access)  
✓ Number of revisions stated clearly  
✓ Timeline included (start date and estimated delivery)  
✓ Deposit percentage and payment terms stated  
✓ How to accept explained ("Reply YES" or "Sign and return")  
✓ Scope change clause included (additional work quoted separately)  
✓ Your contact details and business name on the document  
✓ Proofread for typos, broken numbers, and unclear wording  

**If the client accepts the quote, what happens next?**

1. Send a [deposit invoice](/blog/deposit-invoice-template-uk) (e.g. 50% of total)  
2. Send a [contract](/blog/freelance-contract-template-guide) covering payment terms, IP ownership, liability, and termination  
3. Once deposit clears and contract is signed, schedule the kickoff  
4. Confirm the project start date and delivery milestones  
5. Get client materials (copy, images, access) before starting work  

Do not start work until deposit and contract are done. A quote is not a contract — it is a price agreement. The contract protects both sides if things go wrong.
