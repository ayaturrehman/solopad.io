## What a PO number is (and how it differs from your invoice number)

A **PO number** (short for **purchase order number**) is the client's internal reference for their approval to buy your services. When a client raises a purchase order, their finance or procurement team assigns it a unique number (the PO number) and records what they are buying, from whom, for how much, and by when.

**Example PO numbers:** `PO-45892`, `ORDER-2026-0312`, `PO/2026/1042`, `GPC-20261015-01`

The PO number appears on the purchase order document (a PDF or email the client sends you) and in the client's accounts system. When you invoice the client, you must quote the PO number on your invoice. The client's finance team (or their automated system) matches the PO number on your invoice to the PO in their system. If the numbers match and the invoice amount matches the PO value, payment is approved. If the PO number is missing or wrong, the invoice is rejected.

---

### PO number vs invoice number vs payment reference: what is what

These are NOT the same. Here is what each means.

**Your invoice number:**

✓ Your unique identifier for the invoice you send  
✓ Set by you (e.g. `INV-2026-042`)  
✓ Required by HMRC for your records and VAT compliance  
✓ Always on your invoice (top section, near the date)

For full guidance on invoice numbering, see [invoice number UK](/blog/invoice-number-uk).

**PO number (purchase order number):**

✓ The client's reference for their purchase approval  
✓ Set by the client (e.g. `PO-45892`)  
✓ You include it on your invoice if the client provides one  
✓ Required by many clients (especially NHS trusts, councils, large companies) for payment processing

**Payment reference:**

✓ The reference the client puts in the bank transfer when they pay you  
✓ Often (but not always) the same as your invoice number or their PO number  
✓ You specify it on your invoice (e.g. "Please use invoice number INV-2026-042 as your payment reference")

For more on payment references, see [remittance advice UK](/blog/remittance-advice-uk).

---

### Who issues the PO?

**The client (the buyer)** raises the purchase order. You (the supplier) receive it.

The process works like this:

1. You send the client a quote or proposal for your services (e.g. £2,400 + VAT for a website build)
2. The client's finance or procurement team reviews your quote
3. If they approve it, they create a purchase order in their system
4. The PO records: your business name, the project description, the agreed amount (including VAT if applicable), the PO number, and any terms or conditions
5. The client sends you the PO (usually by email or via a supplier portal like Tradeshift, Coupa, or SAP Ariba)
6. You start work once you have the PO
7. When the work is complete, you invoice the client and quote the PO number on your invoice
8. The client's accounts team matches the invoice to the PO and processes payment

**Key point:** You do NOT create or control the PO. The client does. Your job is to ask for it before you start work and quote it correctly on your invoice.

## "No PO, No Pay" policies — NHS trusts, councils, and large companies

Many UK public sector organisations and large private companies operate a **"No PO, No Pay"** policy. This means:

✗ No valid purchase order number on your invoice = invoice returned unpaid  
✗ Wrong PO number = invoice rejected  
✗ PO expired or value exceeded = invoice rejected  

The policy is not optional. If the client tells you they operate No PO No Pay (or if you see it on their supplier guidance page), you MUST get a PO before you start work. Otherwise you risk doing the work and not being paid for weeks while you fix the invoice.

## Step-by-step: how to get a PO before you start work

The safest way to avoid invoice problems is to get the PO **before you do the work**. Here is how.

### Step 1: Ask if a PO is required (before you quote or accept the job)

When a new client contacts you (or when you pitch for work), ask early:

✓ "Will this project need a purchase order?"  
✓ "Do you operate a No PO No Pay policy?"  
✓ "If a PO is required, who raises it and when will I receive it?"

**When to assume a PO is required:**

- NHS trusts, hospitals, and healthcare organisations  
- Local councils and public bodies  
- Universities and schools  
- Large companies (500+ employees)  
- Any client that mentions procurement, supplier portals, or purchase approval in their emails

---

### Step 2: Confirm PO details before you start work

Once the client confirms a PO is required, ask:

✓ **Who raises the PO?** (Usually the finance team or procurement)  
✓ **What is the PO number?** (Ask them to send you the PO document or email you the PO number)  
✓ **What is the PO value?** (Check it matches your quote, including VAT if applicable)  
✓ **What is the PO end date or expiry date?** (Some POs are valid only until a certain date or financial year-end)  
✓ **Does the PO cover all phases or just the first invoice?** (For phased projects or retainers, clarify whether you need one PO or multiple)

**Example email:**

```
Subject: PO required before we begin — [Project name]

Hi [Client name],

Thanks for confirming you would like to proceed with [project description] at £[amount] + VAT.

Before I start work, please can you provide the purchase order number? I understand your organisation operates a No PO No Pay policy, so I want to make sure I have the correct PO to quote on my invoice.

Please confirm:

✓ The PO number  
✓ The PO value (should be £[amount] + VAT = £[total])  
✓ Any expiry or end date on the PO  

Once I have the PO, I will begin work and keep you updated on progress.

Thanks,  
[Your name]
```

**Copy and adapt this email.** Polite, professional, protects you.

---

### Step 3: Check the PO document when it arrives

When the client sends you the PO (PDF, email, or supplier portal notification), check it line by line:

✓ **Your business name is correct** — Matches your invoice name (your trading name or limited company name)  
✓ **PO description matches your quote** — The work described on the PO matches what you agreed to do  
✓ **PO value matches your quote** — Net amount, VAT, and gross total are correct  
✓ **VAT treatment is correct** — If you are VAT registered, the PO should include VAT at 20% (or the correct rate for your service). If you are not VAT registered, the PO should not include VAT.  
✓ **Payment terms are stated** — E.g. "Payment within 30 days of valid invoice"  
✓ **PO number is clear** — You can read it and will quote it exactly on your invoice  
✓ **Expiry date (if any)** — Note the date. If the PO expires before you finish the work, ask the client to extend it or issue a new PO.

**If anything is wrong, query it immediately.** Do not start work until the PO is correct.

**Example query email:**

```
Subject: Query on PO [PO number] — VAT amount

Hi [Client name],

Thanks for the purchase order (PO-45892).

I notice the PO shows £2,400 + £0 VAT. However, I am VAT registered (VAT number GB 987 6543 21), so the correct VAT is £2,400 × 20% = £480.

The total should be £2,880 (£2,400 + £480 VAT).

Could you amend the PO to include VAT, or let me know if there is a reason the PO does not include VAT?

I am ready to start once the PO is correct.

Thanks,  
[Your name]
```

---

### Step 4: Do not start work without a valid PO

If the client says "We will raise the PO next week, but can you start now?" — say no.

**Why:**

- NHS trusts, councils, and large companies state clearly: supplying without a PO is at your own risk  
- If the PO is delayed or never raised, you have no proof that the client approved the work  
- You may do the work and then have no PO to quote on your invoice, which means no payment

**Polite reply:**

```
Hi [Client name],

I understand you are working on raising the PO. Unfortunately, my terms require a valid purchase order before I begin work, in line with your organisation's own No PO No Pay policy.

Once the PO is raised and you send me the PO number, I will start immediately.

Thanks for understanding.

[Your name]
```

**Exception:** If you have a long-standing relationship with the client and you trust them to raise the PO within 48 hours, you can start at your own risk. But get the PO number in writing as soon as it is raised.

## Where and how to put the PO number on your invoice

Once you have the PO and the work is complete, you invoice the client. Here is where and how to quote the PO number.

### Where: Invoice header section

Put the PO number in the **header** of your invoice, near your invoice number and invoice date.

**Good placement:**

```
Invoice Number: INV-2026-042  
Invoice Date: 15 October 2026  
Your PO Number: PO-45892  
Due Date: 29 October 2026 (Net 14)
```

Or:

```
Invoice: INV-2026-042  
Date: 15 October 2026  
Purchase Order: PO-45892  
Payment Terms: Net 14 days
```

**Bad placement:**

✗ Buried in the footer or notes section (the accounts team may miss it)  
✗ In the middle of the line items (confusing)  
✗ Not labelled (just writing "PO-45892" without a label)

**Label it clearly:**

- "Your PO Number:"  
- "Purchase Order:"  
- "PO Ref:"  
- "Client PO:"

Some clients specify the exact label to use. Follow their instructions.

---

### How: Exact match, no variations

**Quote the PO number exactly as it appears on the PO document.**

✓ If the PO says `PO-45892`, write `PO-45892` on your invoice  
✓ If the PO says `ORDER-2026-0312`, write `ORDER-2026-0312`  
✓ If the PO says `GPC/2026/1042`, write `GPC/2026/1042`

**Do NOT:**

✗ Abbreviate or shorten it  
✗ Change the format (e.g. writing "PO 45892" when the PO says "PO-45892")  
✗ Add extra zeros or letters  
✗ Guess if you are not sure — ask the client to confirm the exact format

Automated invoice-matching systems are strict. One wrong character and your invoice will be rejected.

---

### One PO per invoice (unless told otherwise)

**Default rule:** Use one PO per invoice.

**Example:**

- PO-45892 for Invoice INV-2026-042 (£2,400 + VAT)  
- PO-45893 for Invoice INV-2026-043 (£1,800 + VAT)

**Exception:** If the client tells you to use the same PO for multiple invoices (e.g. a retainer PO that covers three months), follow their instruction. But clarify:

- How many invoices can you raise against the PO?  
- What is the total PO value?  
- Do you need to split the PO value across invoices, or can you invoice as work is completed?

**Never split one invoice across multiple POs unless the client specifically asks you to.**

---

### Line items should mirror the PO description

The line items on your invoice should match the description and value on the PO.

**Example:**

**PO says:**

- Freelance graphic design services — brand refresh  
- Value: £2,400 + VAT  

**Your invoice should say:**

- Freelance graphic design services — brand refresh  
- £2,400.00  
- VAT @ 20%: £480.00  
- Total: £2,880.00

**Do NOT:**

✗ Invoice for work not covered by the PO (this is a scope change — see next section)  
✗ Change the description significantly (e.g. PO says "brand refresh" and you write "logo design and website mock-ups" — this may cause the invoice to be rejected)

If the work you did is slightly different from the PO description, check with the client before invoicing.

---

### Sending the invoice: where does it go?

**Check the PO document** for the invoice submission address or portal.

**Common scenarios:**

1. **Email to a specific address** — E.g. "Send invoices to: accounts@nhstrust.nhs.uk" → Send the invoice PDF to that address, with the PO number in the email subject and body
2. **Supplier portal** — E.g. Tradeshift, Coupa, SAP Ariba, Amazon Business → Log in to the portal and upload your invoice, quoting the PO number
3. **Client's finance team** — The PO specifies a finance contact → Email the invoice to that person

**Never send an invoice to the project contact (the person you did the work for) if the PO specifies a different invoice address.** Invoices sent to the wrong address are delayed or lost.

**Include the PO number in the email subject:**

```
Subject: Invoice INV-2026-042 — PO-45892 — [Your business name]

Hi [Finance team],

Please find attached Invoice INV-2026-042 for freelance graphic design services (brand refresh) as per Purchase Order PO-45892.

Invoice amount: £2,880.00 (£2,400 + £480 VAT)  
Payment terms: Net 14 (due 29 October 2026)

Please let me know if you need any further information.

Thanks,  
[Your name]  
[Your business name]  
[Contact details]
```

## When it goes wrong: common PO problems and how to fix them

Even if you follow all the steps, PO problems still happen. Here is how to fix the most common issues.

### Problem 1: Invoice rejected for missing or wrong PO number

**What happened:**

You sent an invoice. Days or weeks later, you receive an email or automated message: "Invoice rejected. No valid purchase order number."

**Possible causes:**

✗ You forgot to include the PO number on the invoice  
✗ You quoted the wrong PO (typo, or you used a PO from a previous project)  
✗ The PO was cancelled or closed before you invoiced  
✗ The client never raised a PO (you did the work without one)

**How to fix it:**

1. **Contact the client immediately:** "My invoice was rejected due to a missing/incorrect PO. Please can you provide the correct PO number?"
2. **Cancel or credit the incorrect invoice:** Mark the rejected invoice as VOID in your records. If required, issue a credit note (see [credit note template UK](/blog/credit-note-template-uk) for guidance).
3. **Reissue a corrected invoice with the correct PO number:** Use a new invoice number (e.g. if the original was INV-2026-042, the corrected invoice is INV-2026-043). State clearly in the email: "This replaces rejected invoice INV-2026-042."
4. **Resend to the correct invoice address** with the PO number in the subject line

**Prevent it next time:** Always get the PO before you start work, and double-check the PO number on your invoice before you send it.

---

### Problem 2: PO value used up or scope grew

**What happened:**

You invoiced £3,000 but the PO value is only £2,400. The invoice is rejected because it exceeds the PO value.

Or: you completed additional work beyond the original scope (client asked for extra pages, revisions, or features) and you invoiced for the extra, but the PO does not cover it.

**Why this is a problem:**

POs are approval limits. The client's accounts system will not pay an invoice that exceeds the PO value, even if the work was done and approved by the project contact.

**How to fix it:**

1. **Do NOT invoice for work beyond the PO value without a PO amendment or new PO.**
2. **Contact the client before you do extra work:** "The original PO (PO-45892) covers £2,400 for the brand refresh. You have now asked for [additional work]. This is outside the original scope. Can you raise a PO amendment or a new PO for the extra £600?"
3. **Wait for the client to amend the PO or raise a new PO.**
4. **Invoice the original scope against the original PO, and invoice the extra work against the new/amended PO.**

**Example:**

- Original PO: PO-45892, £2,400 + VAT  
- Invoice 1: INV-2026-042, £2,400 + VAT, quoting PO-45892 ✓  
- Client requests extra work (£600 + VAT)  
- Client raises new PO: PO-45910, £600 + VAT  
- Invoice 2: INV-2026-043, £600 + VAT, quoting PO-45910 ✓

**Prevent it next time:** Agree scope clearly upfront. Use a scope of work document (see [freelance scope of work template](/blog/freelance-scope-of-work-template)) and tell the client "Any changes to scope will require a new or amended PO."

---

### Problem 3: PO expired or end date passed

**What happened:**

You finished the work and invoiced, but the PO has an expiry date or financial year-end date that has already passed. The invoice is rejected because the PO is closed.

**Why this happens:**

Public sector organisations often close POs at the end of the financial year (31 March for most UK public bodies). If you invoice after 31 March for work done before 31 March, the old PO may be closed and you need a new PO.

**How to fix it:**

1. **Check the PO expiry date when you receive the PO** (before you start work).
2. **If the PO is close to expiry, ask the client:** "This PO expires on 31 March. My invoice will be dated [April]. Can you extend the PO or raise a new PO for the new financial year?"
3. **If the PO has already expired when you invoice, ask the client to raise a new PO** or reopen the old one.

**Prevent it next time:** Invoice as soon as you complete the work. Do not wait weeks. If a project spans a financial year-end, ask the client upfront: "Will I need a new PO after 31 March?"

---

### Problem 4: PO raised after the work

**What happened:**

The client asked you to start work urgently. You started without a PO (against your better judgment). The work is now complete, but the client is taking weeks to raise the PO and you cannot invoice.

**Why this is a problem:**

You have done the work, but you have no PO to quote on your invoice. The client's finance team will reject any invoice without a PO. You are at the client's mercy.

**How to fix it:**

1. **Chase the PO politely but firmly:** "I completed [project] on [date]. I cannot invoice without a valid purchase order number. Please can you provide the PO by [deadline]?"
2. **Escalate if the client is unresponsive:** Contact their finance or procurement team directly (if you have their details). Explain: "I was asked to start work without a PO. The work is complete and approved by [project contact]. I need a PO to raise my invoice."
3. **If the client refuses to raise a PO, you may need to treat the work as disputed or write it off.** Consult a solicitor or use debt recovery options (see [letter before action template UK](/blog/letter-before-action-template-uk)).

**Prevent it next time:** Never start work without a PO when the client operates No PO No Pay. If the client pressures you to start before the PO is raised, say no and explain the risk.

---

### Problem 5: Client's PO terms differ from your terms

**What happened:**

You send the client your quote with a link to your standard terms of business (Net 14, liability cap, IP transfer on payment). The client raises a PO and sends it to you. The PO includes the client's own standard purchase terms (Net 90, no liability cap, IP transfers on delivery not payment, or an indemnity clause that makes you liable for the client's mistakes).

**Why this is a problem:**

If you accept the PO and start work, you may be agreeing to the client's terms, not yours. This is the **battle of the forms** problem in contract law. Whose terms govern? In many cases, the last set of terms sent before work begins wins.

**How to fix it:**

1. **Read the PO terms carefully** when the client sends you the PO.
2. **If the PO terms conflict with your terms, do NOT accept the PO without flagging it.** Email the client: "Thank you for PO-45892. I note that the PO includes your organisation's standard terms, which differ from my standard terms (linked in my original quote). Before I begin work, please confirm which terms apply to this project, or let's agree any changes in writing."
3. **Agree in writing which terms govern.** Options:
   - Your terms apply (the client agrees to waive their PO terms for this project)  
   - The client's terms apply (you accept this, but only if you are comfortable with their payment terms, liability, and IP clauses)  
   - Hybrid: your terms apply except for specific clauses that the client requires (e.g. you agree to their insurance requirements but keep your own payment terms)

**For high-value or complex projects, consult a solicitor.** Do not sign or accept a PO with terms you do not understand.

**Legal disclaimer:** This is general information, not legal advice. Contract terms and the battle of the forms are complex. For large contracts or unfamiliar terms, seek professional legal advice.

## Getting paid on time with a PO on your invoice

Once your invoice has a valid PO and is submitted correctly, payment should follow the client's stated payment terms (usually Net 30 for public sector, Net 14 or Net 30 for private companies).

### The payment clock starts when a valid invoice is received

**Important:** For many organisations, the payment term (e.g. 30 days) starts from the date they receive a **valid** invoice, not from the date you send it.

**Valid invoice = invoice with correct PO + correct value + correct format + sent to the correct address.**

If your invoice is rejected and you resubmit it, the 30-day clock resets from the date they receive the corrected invoice.

**Example:**

- You send invoice on 1 October (no PO)  
- Invoice rejected on 7 October  
- You resubmit corrected invoice with PO on 10 October  
- Payment clock starts 10 October, due 9 November (Net 30)

**Lesson:** Get the PO right the first time. Every rejection delays payment.

---

### Chase politely if payment is late

If the payment due date passes and you have not been paid, chase politely.

**Email template:**

```
Subject: Payment reminder — Invoice INV-2026-042 (PO-45892)

Hi [Finance team / client name],

I hope you are well. I am writing to follow up on Invoice INV-2026-042 (dated 15 October 2026), which quoted your Purchase Order PO-45892.

```

**Send reminders at:**

- Due date + 7 days (first polite reminder)  
- Due date + 14 days (second reminder, firmer tone)  
- Due date + 30 days (formal notice, mention late payment rights)

For full guidance on chasing late invoices, see [how to chase a late invoice](/blog/how-to-chase-a-late-invoice).

### Your late payment rights (business clients only)

If the client is a business (not an individual consumer), you have legal rights under the **Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998**:

✓ **Statutory interest:** 8% per annum + Bank of England base rate, calculated daily from the due date  
✓ **Fixed compensation:** £40 (invoices up to £999.99), £70 (£1,000-£9,999.99), £100 (£10,000+)  
✓ **Reasonable debt recovery costs:** Solicitor fees, court costs (if legal action is required)

**Important:** The payment due date is the date stated on your invoice (e.g. Net 14 or Net 30), NOT the date on the PO. If your invoice says "Payment due 29 October 2026" and payment arrives on 15 November, you can charge late payment interest and compensation for the 17-day delay.

For full details, see [statutory interest UK freelancers](/blog/statutory-interest-uk-freelancers) and [invoice payment terms UK](/blog/invoice-payment-terms-uk).

**Note:** Late payment rights do NOT depend on the PO. They apply to any business-to-business invoice. The PO is just the reference that allows the client's system to process the invoice. Your payment terms are set by you, not by the PO.

## PO checklist: before you start work and before you invoice

Use this checklist to avoid PO problems.

### Before you start work:

✓ Ask the client: "Do you need a purchase order for this work?"  
✓ If yes, ask: "Who raises the PO and when will I receive it?"  
✓ Wait for the PO to arrive before you start work (unless you have a long-standing relationship and you trust the client to raise it within 48 hours)  
✓ Check the PO when it arrives: your name, description, value, VAT, PO number, expiry date  
✓ Query any errors immediately before you start work  
✓ Save a copy of the PO in your client folder  
✓ Note the PO number in your project notes or task tracker  

---

### Before you invoice:

✓ Confirm the PO is still valid (not expired or closed)  
✓ Check the PO value covers your invoice amount (net + VAT)  
✓ Quote the PO number exactly as it appears on the PO document  
✓ Put the PO number in the invoice header, clearly labelled  
✓ Match your invoice description to the PO description  
✓ Check the invoice submission address (email or portal) on the PO  
✓ Include the PO number in the email subject line when you send the invoice  
✓ Keep a copy of the invoice and PO together in your records
