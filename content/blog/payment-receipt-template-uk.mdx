## What a payment receipt is (and how it differs from an invoice)

A **payment receipt** confirms that payment has been received. An **invoice** requests payment before it arrives.

**Simple distinction:**

✓ **Invoice:** "You owe me £1,200. Please pay by 14 October."  
✓ **Receipt:** "I received £1,200 from you on 14 October. Thank you."

### Invoice vs receipt vs paid invoice — comparison table

| | **Invoice** | **Paid invoice** | **Separate receipt** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Purpose** | Request payment | Confirm payment settled | Confirm payment received |
| **Timing** | Before payment | After payment | After payment |
| **What it shows** | Amount owed | Amount paid (marked PAID) | Amount received |
| **When to use** | Every time you bill a client | When client pays in full | Deposits, part payments, cash, client requests proof |
| **VAT invoice?** | Yes (if VAT registered) | Yes (same invoice) | Not required unless VAT receipt needed |
| **HMRC requirement** | Must issue for taxable supply | Keep as paid | Keep as payment proof |

**When a paid invoice is enough:**

If the client pays the full invoice amount and you mark the invoice PAID (or RECEIPTED) and send it back to them, that acts as a receipt. No separate receipt needed.

**When you need a separate receipt:**

✓ Client paid a **deposit** (50% upfront, rest on delivery)  
✓ Client paid a **part payment** or instalment (£1,200 of £3,000 invoice)  
✓ Client paid **cash** and needs written proof  
✓ Client's **finance team** explicitly requests a receipt document (common in larger organisations)  
✓ Payment covers **multiple invoices** (one payment settles three overdue invoices — issue one receipt listing all three)  
✓ Client paid from a **different entity** (their parent company paid your invoice to their subsidiary — issue a receipt to whoever paid)  

**Bottom line:** A paid invoice can act as a receipt. But for partial payments, cash, or when explicitly requested, issue a standalone receipt.

## When UK freelancers should issue a payment receipt

Not every payment needs a separate receipt. Use this decision tree to decide.

### Scenario 1: Client paid the full invoice amount by bank transfer

**What you owe them:**

Mark the invoice PAID and resend it. Or send a short email: "Thank you for your payment of £[amount] received [date]. Invoice [INV-XXX] is now settled in full."

**Do you need a separate receipt?**

Only if the client's finance team asks for one. Otherwise, the paid invoice (with bank transfer reference on their statement) is sufficient proof.

**Why:** Bank transfer records are legally recognised proof of payment in the UK. A separate receipt adds no extra legal weight, but some client accounting systems require a receipt document to close the invoice.

### Scenario 2: Client paid a deposit (upfront payment before project starts)

**What you owe them:**

Issue a deposit receipt showing: amount received, invoice number, date, remaining balance, when the balance is due.

**Why:** The invoice is only half-settled. A receipt makes it clear what was paid (the deposit) and what remains (the balance). This prevents disputes when you invoice the final amount.

**Example:** You invoiced £2,400. Client paid £1,200 deposit. Issue a receipt confirming £1,200 received, £1,200 remaining balance to be invoiced on delivery.

### Scenario 3: Client paid a part payment or instalment

**What you owe them:**

Issue a part payment receipt showing: amount paid, invoice number, date, balance remaining, next payment due date.

**Why:** Same reason as deposits — partial payments need clear tracking. The client (and their accountant) need to know how much they have paid and how much is left.

**Example:** You invoiced £3,000 for a retainer. Client paid £1,200 as month 1 instalment. Issue a receipt confirming £1,200 received, £1,800 remaining over two further instalments.

### Scenario 4: Client paid cash

**What you owe them:**

Issue a cash receipt immediately. If VAT registered and the amount is over £250, the receipt must meet VAT receipt rules (see VAT section below).

**Why:** Cash has no bank trail. The receipt is the only proof the client paid you (and that you received it). If they lose the receipt, they cannot prove payment. And if HMRC audits you, cash receipts are required.

**Important:** If you are a sole trader or limited company, you must declare cash income. A receipt protects both you and the client.

### Scenario 5: Client is a consumer (non-business) and paid by card or PayPal

**What you owe them:**

Provide a receipt or payment confirmation. Consumer Contracts Regulations do not mandate a receipt for services, but it is good practice and expected for consumer transactions.

**Why:** Consumers expect receipts. Even though card statements show the payment, a receipt with your business name, the service provided, and the amount builds trust and reduces disputes.

### Scenario 6: Client paid from a different entity or account

**What you owe them:**

Issue a receipt to the entity that paid, referencing the invoice issued to the original client.

**Example:** You invoiced Oak Digital Ltd. Their parent company (Oak Group Holdings Ltd) paid your invoice. Issue a receipt to Oak Group Holdings confirming they settled Oak Digital's invoice.

**Why:** The entity that paid needs proof they settled a debt. Your invoice was to one entity, but payment came from another — a receipt makes the chain clear.

### Scenario 7: One payment settles multiple invoices

**What you owe them:**

Issue one receipt listing all invoices the payment settles, the amount allocated to each, and any remaining balance.

**Example:** Client owed you £800 (Invoice 101), £1,200 (Invoice 102), and £600 (Invoice 103) = £2,600 total. They paid £2,000. Issue a receipt allocating £800 to 101 (settled), £1,200 to 102 (settled), and £0 to 103 (£600 still owed).

**Why:** Without a receipt, the client (and you) may disagree on which invoices the payment covered. A receipt makes the allocation explicit.

### When you do NOT need a receipt

✓ Client paid a full invoice by bank transfer and does not ask for a receipt (the paid invoice is proof)  
✓ You use Stripe, PayPal, or payment software that auto-generates a receipt (the platform receipt is sufficient)  
✓ Invoice is still unpaid (you cannot receipt money you have not received)  

**Bottom line:** Deposits, part payments, cash, and explicit client requests = issue a receipt. Full payment by bank transfer with no request = paid invoice is usually enough.

## What to include on a UK payment receipt — field checklist

A payment receipt is simpler than an invoice, but it must still meet basic business and tax record requirements.

### Required fields for all UK payment receipts

✓ **"Receipt" or "Payment Receipt"** label at the top (not "Invoice")  
✓ **Unique receipt number** (e.g. REC-2026-012 — separate sequence from invoices)  
✓ **Date payment was received** (not the date you issue the receipt — the date the money arrived)  
✓ **Your business name**  
✓ **Your address** (for HMRC purposes; can be abbreviated)  
✓ **Your contact details** (email or phone)  
✓ **Client name** (or payer name if different from the client you invoiced)  
✓ **Invoice number(s)** the payment settles or part-settles  
✓ **Description** (e.g. "Deposit — 50% of website build project" or "Part payment — retainer month 1")  
✓ **Amount received** in GBP (net if VAT registered and you are showing VAT separately)  
✓ **Payment method** (bank transfer, cash, card, cheque)  
✓ **Balance outstanding** (if the invoice is not fully paid)  
✓ **Confirmation wording** (e.g. "Received with thanks" or "This receipt confirms payment")

### VAT-registered freelancers — additional VAT receipt fields

If you are VAT registered and issuing a VAT receipt, you must show:

✓ **Your VAT number**  
✓ **Net amount**  
✓ **VAT amount** (at 20% standard rate unless zero-rated or exempt)  
✓ **Gross total** (net + VAT)  

**However:** A receipt for a deposit or part payment is **not required to be a full VAT invoice**. Your original invoice is the VAT invoice. The receipt is just proof of payment. You can issue a simplified receipt without VAT breakdown, as long as the VAT was on the original invoice.

**Exception:** If you are issuing a receipt for cash over £250 and you are VAT registered, the receipt **must** include VAT details (VAT Notice 700, para 16.3.3).

**Source:** [GOV.UK — VAT invoices (VAT Notice 700/12)](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-invoices)

### Optional helpful fields

✓ **Project name or reference** (so the client can file the receipt with the project)  
✓ **Next payment due date** (for part payments or instalments)  
✓ **Payment reference** the client used (so you can match the receipt to the bank transfer)  
✓ **Your bank details** (if the client might forward the receipt to others who may pay you)  
✓ **Thank you note** (builds goodwill)

**What you do NOT need:**

✗ Payment terms (the receipt confirms payment already made — payment terms are on the invoice)  
✗ Late payment interest or fees (those go on invoices or statutory interest demands, not receipts)  
✗ Detailed line items (the invoice has those — the receipt is a summary)  

**Bottom line:** Receipt number, date received, client, invoice reference, amount, payment method, balance (if any), and VAT details (if VAT registered and required). Keep it simple.

## VAT on payment receipts — what VAT registered freelancers need to know

If you are VAT registered, your **invoice** is your VAT invoice. A **receipt** is just proof of payment. You do not need to issue a full VAT invoice every time you confirm payment.

### Simplified receipts for deposits and part payments

If you are VAT registered and the client pays a deposit or part payment, you can issue a **simplified receipt** without full VAT invoice details, as long as:

✓ Your original invoice is a valid VAT invoice (shows your VAT number, net, VAT, gross)  
✓ The receipt references the original invoice number  
✓ The receipt shows the gross amount received  

**Example simplified receipt:**

> Received £1,440 from Oak Digital Ltd on 14 October 2026 for deposit on invoice INV-2026-089 (50% of £2,880 gross). Balance remaining: £1,440. Thank you.

No VAT breakdown needed on the receipt if the original invoice is a proper VAT invoice.

### When you MUST show VAT on a receipt

If you are VAT registered and **any** of these apply, the receipt must show net, VAT, and gross:

✓ The payment is **cash over £250** (VAT Notice 700, para 16.3.3 — cash receipts over £250 require VAT details)  
✓ The receipt is being issued **instead of an invoice** (e.g. client paid before you invoiced — issue a VAT receipt that acts as both receipt and VAT invoice)  
✓ The client **requests a VAT receipt** explicitly  

**Cash over £250 VAT receipt example:**

> **Receipt REC-2026-050**  
> Received from Oak Digital Ltd: **Cash £300.00**  
> For: Consulting session (Invoice INV-2026-098)  
> Date received: 14 October 2026  
> Net: £250.00  
> VAT @ 20%: £50.00  
> Gross: £300.00  
> VAT number: GB 234 5678 90  

**Source:** [GOV.UK — VAT Guide (Notice 700)](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-guide-notice-700), [VAT Invoices (Notice 700/12)](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/vat-invoices), and [Cash accounting scheme (Notice 731)](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/use-the-vat-cash-accounting-scheme-notice-731).

### If you are NOT VAT registered

Do not show VAT on the receipt. Write "Not VAT registered" or leave the VAT line blank. Only VAT-registered businesses charge VAT.

**VAT registration threshold (as of April 2024):** You must register for VAT if your taxable turnover exceeds **£90,000** in a rolling 12-month period.

For full UK VAT registration guidance, read [when to register for VAT as a UK freelancer](/blog/when-to-register-for-vat-uk-freelancers).

## HMRC record keeping — how long to keep payment receipts

UK freelancers must keep payment receipts as part of their business records. HMRC can ask to see them during an enquiry or audit.

### Sole traders (self-employed)

You must keep records for **at least 5 years after the 31 January submission deadline** of the relevant tax year.

**Example:**

- Tax year: 2025/26 (6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026)  
- Self Assessment deadline: 31 January 2027  
- Keep records until: 31 January 2032 (5 years after the deadline)  

**What to keep:**

✓ All invoices (sent and received)  
✓ All payment receipts (issued and received)  
✓ Bank statements showing payments in and out  
✓ Cash book or accounting records  
✓ Expense receipts  

**Source:** [GOV.UK — Business records if you're self-employed](https://www.gov.uk/self-employed-records)

### Limited companies

Directors of limited companies must keep records for **at least 6 years** from the end of the accounting period.

**Example:**

- Accounting period: 1 January 2026 to 31 December 2026  
- Keep records until: 31 December 2032 (6 years after period end)  

**What to keep:**

✓ All invoices, receipts, and payment records  
✓ Bank statements and accounting records  
✓ VAT records (if VAT registered)  
✓ Corporation Tax records  

**Source:** [GOV.UK — Company records you must keep](https://www.gov.uk/running-a-limited-company/company-and-accounting-records)

### VAT registered (sole trader or limited company)

If you are VAT registered, you must keep VAT records for **6 years**.

**What to keep:**

✓ VAT invoices (sales and purchases)  
✓ VAT receipts (cash receipts over £250 must show VAT)  
✓ VAT returns and supporting calculations  
✓ Import/export records (if applicable)  

**Source:** [GOV.UK — VAT record keeping](https://www.gov.uk/vat-record-keeping)

### Best practice — how to file receipts

✓ Number receipts sequentially (REC-2026-001, REC-2026-002, etc.)  
✓ Save a copy of every receipt you issue (PDF or paper)  
✓ File receipts with the corresponding invoice (so HMRC can see invoice + payment + receipt together)  
✓ Back up digital records (cloud storage or external drive)  
✓ Keep a cash book or accounting spreadsheet showing: date, client, invoice number, amount received, payment method  

**Digital records are fine.** HMRC accepts scanned receipts, PDFs, and accounting software records. You do not need to keep paper copies unless you prefer.

**Tip:** If you use accounting software (Xero, QuickBooks, FreeAgent), it auto-generates receipts when you mark invoices as paid. Download the receipts as PDFs and store them.

## Common mistakes that confuse clients (and fail HMRC checks)

**Calling a receipt an "invoice."** A receipt is not an invoice. Label it "Receipt" or "Payment Receipt" at the top. If you call it an invoice, the client's accounting system may treat it as a second invoice (duplicate).

**Not referencing the invoice number.** Always state which invoice the payment settles. Otherwise, the client cannot match the receipt to the invoice, and HMRC cannot trace the payment.

**Issuing a receipt before payment arrives.** Do not issue a receipt until the money is in your bank account. "I sent it this morning" is not the same as "It has arrived." Wait for cleared funds.

**Not showing the balance remaining.** For part payments or deposits, always state how much remains unpaid. Otherwise, the client may think the full invoice is settled.

**Issuing a VAT receipt when you are not VAT registered.** Only VAT-registered businesses show VAT on receipts. If you are not registered, do not add VAT. Write "Not VAT registered" if asked.

**Not keeping a copy.** Save every receipt you issue. HMRC can ask to see them during an enquiry. If you lose the copy, you have no proof you issued the receipt (and the client may claim they never received payment confirmation).

**Using the same numbering sequence for invoices and receipts.** Number receipts separately (e.g. REC-2026-001, REC-2026-002) so they do not conflict with invoice numbers. This keeps your accounting clean.

**Issuing a receipt for a refund.** A receipt confirms money received. If you refund money, issue a **credit note** (which reduces what the client owes) or a refund confirmation (which says you sent money back). Do not call a refund a receipt — that confuses both sides.

## When you should NOT issue a receipt

**The invoice is still unpaid.** Do not issue a receipt until payment has arrived and cleared. A receipt is proof of payment received, not a promise of future payment.

**The client paid via Stripe, PayPal, or payment software.** These platforms auto-generate receipts. You do not need to issue a duplicate receipt unless the client explicitly requests one.

**The payment was a mistake or duplicate.** If the client paid twice by accident, refund the duplicate and send a refund confirmation. Do not issue a receipt for the duplicate payment (it makes it look like you are keeping the money).

**You are issuing a credit note.** A credit note reduces the amount owed. A receipt confirms money received. They are different documents. If you overcharged and are crediting the client, issue a credit note (see our [credit note template guide](/blog/credit-note-template-uk)). If they already paid and you are refunding, issue a refund confirmation (not a receipt).
