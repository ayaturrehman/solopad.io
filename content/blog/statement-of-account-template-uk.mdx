## What a statement of account is (and when UK freelancers should send one)

A **statement of account** (also called an **account statement** or **client statement**) is a document that summarises all financial transactions between you and a client over a period — usually a month, a quarter, or year-to-date.

It shows:

✓ All invoices issued (with dates, invoice numbers, and amounts)  
✓ All payments received (with dates and amounts)  
✓ Any credits or adjustments applied  
✓ A running balance after each transaction  
✓ The current total owed  
✓ Often an **ageing summary** showing how overdue each invoice is  

**Example:** You have worked with a client for three months. You sent five invoices. They paid two, partially paid one, and two are overdue. A statement shows all five invoices in date order, the two payments, the running balance, and the £4,800 they currently owe.

### Statement of account vs invoice vs payment reminder

**Invoice:** A single demand for payment. "You owe £1,200 for this project. Pay by 14 days."

**Payment reminder (chase email):** A follow-up when one invoice is overdue. "Invoice INV-2026-078 (£1,200) was due on 24 September. Please pay now."

**Statement of account:** A summary of multiple invoices and payments. "Here is everything you owe me. Total due: £4,800."

Use a statement when you need to show the full financial relationship, not just one overdue invoice.

## When to send a statement of account — the decision tree

Not every freelancer needs to send statements. Here is when they make sense.

### Send a statement when:

✓ **You have multiple open invoices with one client** (e.g. monthly retainer, ongoing project billing, or several completed projects not yet paid)  
✓ **The client asks "what do I owe?"** (a statement answers that question in one document)  
✓ **You want to summarise before escalating** — send a statement as the final polite reminder before a letter before action  
✓ **You have a monthly billing cycle** with corporate clients who expect statements as part of their accounts payable process  
✓ **The client has paid some invoices but not others** and you want to show the remaining balance clearly  

### Do NOT send a statement when:

✗ You have **one overdue invoice** — just send a chase email directly (see our [how to chase a late invoice guide](/blog/how-to-chase-a-late-invoice))  
✗ The client has **disputed the work** — resolve the dispute first, then invoice or statement  
✗ You are ready to **send a letter before action** — at that stage, send the legal demand, not a soft summary  

**Bottom line:** Statements are for clarity and routine updates. Use them when you need to show multiple transactions. For single overdue invoices, chase directly.

## Statement of account vs chase email vs letter before action — when to use each

You have three tools to get paid. Here is when to use each.

### Use a **payment chase email** when:

✓ One invoice is overdue  
✓ You have sent a polite reminder already  
✓ The invoice is under 30 days overdue  
✓ You want to keep the tone friendly  

**Example email:**

> Hi [Client name],  
> Just a quick reminder that invoice INV-2026-078 (£1,200) was due on 24 September and is now 8 days overdue. Can you confirm when you will send payment? Let me know if you need a copy of the invoice. Thanks, [Your name]

For full chase email templates, see [how to chase a late invoice](/blog/how-to-chase-a-late-invoice).

### Use a **statement of account** when:

✓ You have multiple invoices (paid and unpaid)  
✓ You want to show the total owed  
✓ You are sending a monthly update to an ongoing client  
✓ You want to summarise before escalating  

**Example email:**

> Hi [Client name],  
> Please find attached your statement of account for January–September 2026. The total balance outstanding is £4,800, made up of three overdue invoices. Payment details are on each invoice. Let me know if you have any questions. Thanks, [Your name]

### Use a **letter before action** when:

✓ Chasing has failed  
✓ The invoice is 30+ days overdue  
✓ The client is ignoring you  
✓ You are ready to escalate to court if they do not pay  

**Example opening:**

> This is a formal letter before action regarding unpaid invoices INV-2026-078, INV-2026-081, and INV-2026-083, totalling £4,800. Under the Late Payment of Commercial Debts Act 1998, I am entitled to statutory interest and recovery costs. If I do not receive payment of £4,987 (invoice total + interest + recovery costs) by [deadline], I will issue court proceedings without further notice.

For full letter before action templates, see [letter before action template UK](/blog/letter-before-action-template-uk).

### Decision checklist

✓ **One invoice, under 30 days overdue?** → Chase email  
✓ **One invoice, 30+ days overdue, client ignoring you?** → Letter before action  
✓ **Multiple invoices, client paying some but not others?** → Statement of account  
✓ **Multiple invoices, all overdue, client not responding?** → Statement first, then letter before action if ignored  
✓ **Monthly retainer client?** → Statement every month as routine  

**Tip:** You can send a statement and then escalate. A statement is not a replacement for a letter before action. It is a summary before you get formal.

## What to include on a UK statement of account — field checklist

A good statement of account is clear, detailed, and shows the full picture. Here is what to include.

### Required fields

✓ **"Statement of Account"** label at the top  
✓ **Date range** (e.g. "1 January 2026 – 30 September 2026" or "Year to date as of 30 September 2026")  
✓ **Your business name and address**  
✓ **Your contact details** (email, phone, or both)  
✓ **Client name and address**  
✓ **Statement date** (the date you issued the statement)  
✓ **Opening balance** (if there was a balance brought forward from a previous period)  
✓ **Line-by-line transaction list:**
  - Date of each invoice  
  - Invoice number  
  - Description (brief — e.g. "Website design — Invoice INV-2026-078")  
  - Invoice amount (debit)  
  - Payment amount (credit, shown as negative or in brackets)  
  - Running balance after each transaction  
✓ **Total balance due** (the amount currently owed)  
✓ **Ageing summary** (optional but recommended — breaks down overdue amounts by age: Current / 30 / 60 / 90+ days)  
✓ **Payment instructions** (bank details or how to pay)  

### VAT on statements (VAT registered freelancers)

If you are VAT registered, show VAT on each invoice line or as a separate column. The statement should match your invoices.

**Example:**

| Date | Invoice | Description | Net | VAT | Gross | Payment | Balance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 Aug | INV-078 | Brand design | £1,000 | £200 | £1,200 | | £1,200 |
| 22 Aug | Payment | Bank transfer | | | | (£1,200) | £0 |

If you are **not VAT registered**, just show gross amounts. No VAT column needed.

### Ageing summary — why it matters

An **ageing summary** groups outstanding invoices by how overdue they are. This helps you and the client see which invoices are urgent.

**Standard ageing bands:**

- **Current** — not yet due  
- **30 days** — 1–30 days overdue  
- **60 days** — 31–60 days overdue  
- **90+ days** — over 60 days overdue  

**Example:**

| Age | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Current | £1,200 |
| 30 days | £2,400 |
| 60 days | £0 |
| 90+ days | £1,200 |
| **Total due** | **£4,800** |

This tells you: £1,200 is not yet due, £2,400 is 1–30 days late, and £1,200 is over 60 days late (escalate that one immediately).

We will show you how to build an ageing summary below.

## How to build an ageing summary — step-by-step

An ageing summary shows which invoices are overdue and how urgent each one is. Here is how to build one.

### Step 1: List all unpaid invoices

Make a table with:

- Invoice number  
- Invoice date  
- Due date  
- Amount  
- Days overdue (today's date minus due date)  

**Example (today is 30 September 2026):**

| Invoice | Due date | Amount | Days overdue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| INV-2026-078 | 24 Aug 2026 | £1,400 | 37 days |
| INV-2026-081 | 11 Sep 2026 | £1,600 | 19 days |
| INV-2026-089 | 26 Sep 2026 | £1,400 | 4 days |

### Step 2: Group invoices by age band

Standard bands:

- **Current** — not yet due (due date is in the future)  
- **30 days** — 1–30 days overdue  
- **60 days** — 31–60 days overdue  
- **90+ days** — over 60 days overdue  

For the example above:

- INV-2026-078 (37 days overdue) → **60 days** band  
- INV-2026-081 (19 days overdue) → **30 days** band  
- INV-2026-089 (4 days overdue) → **30 days** band  

### Step 3: Sum each band

| Age | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Current | £0 |
| 30 days | £3,000 (£1,600 + £1,400) |
| 60 days | £1,400 |
| 90+ days | £0 |
| **Total due** | **£4,400** |

### Step 4: Add to your statement

Put the ageing summary at the bottom of your statement, after the transaction list. This makes it easy for the client (and you) to see which invoices need urgent attention.

**Tip:** If you have a 90+ day invoice, escalate immediately. Do not wait for the next monthly statement. Send a [letter before action](/blog/letter-before-action-template-uk) with statutory interest.

## What to do after you send a statement — three outcomes

### Outcome 1 — They pay in full

Job done. Confirm receipt of payment:

> Hi [Client name],  
> Payment of £4,400 received on [Date]. All outstanding invoices are now settled. Thank you.

Mark the invoices as paid in your accounting system. Update your statement file for next time.

### Outcome 2 — They ask for time or dispute something

**If they ask for more time:**

Set a written deadline. Do not leave it vague.

> Hi [Client name],  
> Thanks for your message. I understand cash flow can be tight. Can you confirm a specific date you will send payment? I need the outstanding balance cleared by [Date].

**If they dispute an invoice:**

Ask for written details. Review your contract and delivery proof. Try to resolve it.

> Hi [Client name],  
> Thanks for letting me know. Can you send me written details of what you are disputing? I will review my records and get back to you within [X] days.

If the dispute is a stalling tactic (e.g. they raise quality concerns three months after delivery), hold firm and refer to your contract terms.

### Outcome 3 — They ignore the statement

If the statement is ignored, escalate.

**Next steps:**

1. Send one final chase email referencing the statement  
2. If still no reply, send a formal **letter before action** (see [letter before action template UK](/blog/letter-before-action-template-uk))  
3. If the letter is ignored, file a claim via **Money Claim Online**  

Do not send endless statements. If polite reminders and a statement do not work, escalate to a legal demand.

## When NOT to send a statement of account — alternatives

**You have one overdue invoice and no other unpaid invoices.** Just chase the invoice directly. A statement is unnecessary.

**The client has already ignored multiple reminders.** Skip straight to a letter before action. A statement will not change their behaviour.

**You are ready to start court proceedings.** Do not send a statement at that stage. Send the legal demand (letter before action) with statutory interest and recovery costs.

**The client has disputed the work and you have not resolved it.** Sending a statement will not resolve a quality or scope dispute. Fix the dispute first, then invoice or statement.

**You want to demand immediate payment with interest.** A statement is a summary, not a legal demand. For formal debt recovery with interest, use a letter before action.

**Bottom line:** Statements are for clarity and routine updates. Use them when you have multiple transactions to summarise. For single overdue invoices or formal demands, use chase emails or letters before action instead.

## Common mistakes that confuse clients (and delay payment)

**Not showing a running balance.** A statement without a running balance is just a list of invoices. The running balance shows the client exactly what they owe after each transaction. Always include it.

**Forgetting to subtract payments.** If the client paid £1,200 on 22 August, show it as a credit (negative amount or in brackets) and reduce the balance. Do not just list invoices and ignore payments.

**Mixing up dates.** Use **invoice date** for when the invoice was issued, **due date** for when it was supposed to be paid, and **statement date** for when you issued the statement. Do not confuse them.

**No ageing summary.** Without an ageing summary, the client cannot see which invoices are overdue. Always add ageing if you have multiple unpaid invoices.

**Sending a statement for one invoice.** If you only have one unpaid invoice, just chase it directly. A statement is overkill.

**Using a statement as a legal demand.** A statement is a summary, not a formal demand. If you want to demand payment with interest, send a letter before action, not a statement.

**Not updating the client on credits or adjustments.** If you issued a credit note or applied a discount, show it on the statement. Otherwise, the balance will not match the invoices and the client will be confused.

## Decision checklist — should I send a statement of account for this situation?

Use this checklist to decide whether you need a statement.

**Send a statement if:**

✓ You have multiple invoices with one client (paid and unpaid)  
✓ The client asks "what do I owe?"  
✓ You want to send a monthly update to a retainer or ongoing client  
✓ You have invoices at different overdue stages and want to show the full picture  
✓ You want to summarise before escalating to a letter before action  

**Do NOT send a statement if:**

✗ You have one unpaid invoice (chase it directly)  
✗ The client has already ignored multiple reminders (escalate to letter before action)  
✗ The client has disputed the work and you have not resolved it  
✗ You are ready to start court proceedings (send letter before action instead)  

**Bottom line:** Statements are for clarity and routine updates. Use them to show the full financial relationship, not to chase single invoices or make legal demands.
