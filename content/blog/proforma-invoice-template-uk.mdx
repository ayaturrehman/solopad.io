Your client emails: "Can you send us a proforma invoice for internal approval?"

You pause. You have heard the term before, but you have never sent one. You know it is not a normal invoice. But what is it exactly? Is it a quote? A placeholder? Do you charge VAT on it?

A **proforma invoice** is a document that looks like an invoice but is not a request for payment. It shows what the client will owe *if* they proceed with the work. Procurement teams ask for proforma invoices to get budget approval or issue purchase orders. It is common in corporate and public sector work.

## What a proforma invoice is (and when UK freelancers need one)

A **proforma invoice** (sometimes written as **pro forma invoice**) is an advance invoice that shows what the client will owe if they proceed with the work. It is not a legal demand for payment. It is not an official VAT invoice. It is a fixed estimate formatted to look like an invoice.

**Example use cases:**

✓ Client needs internal budget approval before committing to the project  
✓ Procurement team requires a 'proforma' before they can issue a purchase order  
✓ Client wants a formal estimate that shows exactly what they will pay, including VAT  
✓ Public sector or corporate client has a multi-step approval process  
✓ You are quoting for work that will not start for weeks, and the client needs documentation now  

### What a proforma invoice is not

A proforma is **not**:

✗ A request for payment (that is a deposit invoice or tax invoice)  
✗ A VAT invoice (you must label it "This is not a VAT invoice")  
✗ Legally binding (the client has not committed to pay yet)  
✗ A quote that can change (proforma shows fixed amounts)  

If you want money now, do not send a proforma. Send a deposit invoice or final invoice. A proforma is for clients who need to see what they will owe before they commit.

## Proforma invoice vs quote vs deposit invoice vs tax invoice — the decision tree

Most UK freelancers do not need proforma invoices. You need them only when clients ask for them or when you work with corporate procurement processes.

### Use a **quote** when:

✓ The client is exploring options and wants a rough price  
✓ The scope might change before work starts  
✓ You want to keep it informal  
✓ The client does not need internal approval  

**Example:** "£2,000–£2,500 depending on complexity. Let me know if you want to proceed."

### Use a **proforma invoice** when:

✓ The client asks for a 'proforma' explicitly  
✓ Procurement needs documentation before issuing a purchase order  
✓ The client needs internal approval and a formal document  
✓ You want to give a fixed estimate that looks official but is not yet a request for payment  

**Example:** A corporate client needs sign-off from their finance team before you start. They ask for a proforma invoice. You send it. They approve it. Then you send a deposit invoice to get paid.

### Use a **deposit invoice** when:

✓ The client has agreed to the work  
✓ You want money upfront before starting  
✓ The project is over £1,000 or the client is new  
✓ You need to protect your cashflow  

**Example:** The client approves your proforma. You then send a deposit invoice for 40% (£1,200) due on receipt. Work starts once the deposit clears.

### Use a **tax invoice** (final invoice) when:

✓ Work is complete and you want full payment  
✓ You are invoicing a monthly retainer  
✓ You are invoicing the balance after a deposit  

**Example:** Project is finished. You send the final invoice for the remaining 60% (£1,800) with Net 14 payment terms.

**Key difference:** A proforma says "this is what you will owe if you proceed." A tax invoice says "this is what you owe now, please pay."

## What to include on a UK proforma invoice — field checklist

A proforma invoice looks like a normal invoice, but with two critical differences:

1. It must be labelled **"Proforma Invoice"** at the top (not "Invoice")  
2. It must include the disclaimer **"This is not a VAT invoice"** if you are VAT registered  

### Required fields for a UK proforma invoice

✓ **"Proforma Invoice"** label at the top  
✓ **Proforma reference number** (e.g. PROFORMA-2026-045 — separate sequence from real invoices)  
✓ **Issue date**  
✓ **Validity date** (e.g. "Valid until 30 September 2026" — not a payment due date)  
✓ **Your business name and address**  
✓ **Your contact details** (email or phone)  
✓ **Client name and address**  
✓ **Client billing contact or purchase order number** (if known)  
✓ **Clear description** of services or goods  
✓ **Quantity, rate, and total for each line item**  
✓ **Subtotal**  
✓ **VAT estimate** (if you are VAT registered — label it "Estimated VAT")  
✓ **Total amount** (the amount the client will owe if they proceed)  
✓ **Disclaimer:** "This is not a VAT invoice"  
✓ **Validity statement** (e.g. "This proforma is valid for 30 days from the issue date")  

### What NOT to include

✗ A payment due date (proforma is not a demand for payment)  
✗ Bank account details (they are not paying this — you will send a real invoice later)  
✗ "Payment terms: Net 14" (this is not an invoice yet)  
✗ Your VAT registration number on the header (include it only in the VAT notes if needed)  

**Why the disclaimer matters:** If you send a document that looks like a VAT invoice but is not, HMRC can treat it as a real VAT invoice, and you may owe VAT even if you have not been paid. Always label proforma invoices clearly.

**Source:** [GOV.UK — Invoicing and taking payment from customers](https://www.gov.uk/invoicing-and-taking-payment-from-customers)

## How to convert a proforma invoice to a final tax invoice

When the client approves the proforma and you are ready to request payment, you create a **new invoice** — not edit the proforma.

### Step-by-step conversion process

1. **Do not edit the proforma.** Keep it as a record of what the client approved.  
2. **Create a new invoice** with a new invoice number (use your normal invoice numbering sequence — e.g. INV-2026-089).  
3. **Reference the proforma** in the description (e.g. "As per proforma PROFORMA-2026-045").  
4. **Remove the proforma disclaimers** ("This is not a VAT invoice" — your new invoice is a real VAT invoice if you are VAT registered).  
5. **Add payment terms** (due date, Net 14, payment details).  
6. **Add your bank details** (sort code, account number).  
7. **Book the invoice as accounts receivable** in your accounting system.  
8. **Send the new invoice** to the client.  

### Example — converting a proforma to a deposit invoice

**Original proforma:**

- Reference: PROFORMA-2026-045  
- Total: £2,200  
- Disclaimer: "This is not a VAT invoice"  
- No payment terms  

**New deposit invoice:**

- Invoice number: INV-2026-089  
- Description: "Deposit — 50% of total project fee (£2,200) as per proforma PROFORMA-2026-045"  
- Amount: £1,100  
- Due date: 6 October 2026 (due on receipt)  
- Payment terms: "Payment due on receipt. Work will commence once deposit is received."  
- Bank details: Sort code 04-00-04, Account 12345678  

The client now has a real invoice requesting payment. The proforma stays on file as the original estimate they approved.

### Example — converting a proforma to a final tax invoice (after deposit paid)

If the client paid a deposit and work is now complete, your final invoice references both the proforma and the deposit:

**Invoice INV-2026-090**  
**Date:** 20 October 2026  
**Due date:** 3 November 2026 (Net 14)  
**Project:** Brand identity design — Final invoice  

| Description | Qty | Rate | Amount |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brand identity project (total agreed fee per proforma PROFORMA-2026-045) | 1 | £2,200 | £2,200 |
| Less deposit paid (Invoice INV-2026-089, 6 October 2026) | 1 | -£1,100 | -£1,100 |
| **Balance due** | | | **£1,100** |

**Payment details:**  
Bank transfer to Northline Studio  
Sort code: 04-00-04

**Payment terms:** Net 14. Payment due by 3 November 2026.

## Email scripts — sending a proforma, when clients try to pay it, and why they need a deposit invoice too

### Script 1 — Sending the proforma invoice for approval

**Subject:** Proforma invoice — [Project name]

> Hi [Client name],
>
> Thank you for asking for a proforma invoice. Attached is proforma PROFORMA-2026-045 showing the project cost breakdown (£X,XXX total).
>
> This proforma is for your internal approval process. **It is not a request for payment** — I will send a separate deposit invoice once you confirm you want to proceed.
>
> The proforma is valid for 30 days. If the scope changes, I will issue a revised proforma before we start.
>
> Let me know when you have approval, and I will send the deposit invoice to get us started.
>
> Best,  
> [Your name]

### Script 2 — When a client tries to pay the proforma

Clients sometimes see "invoice" on the proforma and try to pay it. Stop them.

**Subject:** Re: Proforma invoice — [Project name]

> Hi [Client name],
>
> Thank you for your message. The proforma (PROFORMA-2026-045) is for approval purposes only — it is not a request for payment, and you should not pay it.
>
> Now that you have approved the project, I will send you a **deposit invoice** requesting £X,XXX (40% of the total project fee). That is the invoice you should pay.
>
> The deposit invoice will have my bank details and a payment due date. The proforma does not.
>
> I will send the deposit invoice today. Let me know if you have any questions.
>
> Best,  
> [Your name]

### Script 3 — When a client asks why they need a deposit invoice if you already sent a proforma

**Subject:** Re: Deposit invoice — why do I need this if I already have the proforma?

> Hi [Client name],
>
> Great question. The proforma was for your internal approval. Now that you have approved the project, the deposit invoice is the actual request for payment.
>
> The proforma is not a VAT invoice and does not have payment terms or bank details. The deposit invoice is the real invoice that triggers payment and allows me to start work.
>
> Think of the proforma as a fixed quote, and the deposit invoice as the first payment milestone. The proforma shows what you agreed to pay. The deposit invoice asks for the first part of that payment.
>
> Let me know if that makes sense, or if you need me to explain further.
>
> Best,  
> [Your name]

### Script 4 — When a client asks for a proforma invoice but you think they need a deposit invoice instead

Sometimes clients ask for a proforma when they actually just want to pay you. If they are a small business or sole trader and there is no procurement process, they probably do not need a proforma.

**Subject:** Re: Request for proforma invoice

> Hi [Client name],
>
> Thanks for your message. Just to clarify — do you need a proforma invoice for internal approval or procurement, or are you ready to proceed and pay a deposit?
>
> If you are ready to start, I can send you a **deposit invoice** (40% due upfront) and we can begin as soon as that is paid.
>
> If you need internal sign-off first, I am happy to send a proforma invoice for your finance team to approve, and then I will follow up with the deposit invoice once you have approval.
>
> Let me know which works best for you.
>
> Best,  
> [Your name]

This saves time. If they do not need a proforma, you go straight to a deposit invoice and get paid faster.

## VAT on proforma invoices — what to show and what to say

If you are **VAT registered**, you can show estimated VAT on a proforma invoice, but you must make it clear this is not an official VAT invoice.

### How to show VAT on a proforma (VAT registered freelancers)

✓ Label the VAT line as **"Estimated VAT @ 20%"** (not just "VAT")  
✓ Include the disclaimer: **"This is not a VAT invoice. VAT shown is an estimate. The actual VAT invoice will be issued when payment is requested."**  
✓ Do **not** show your VAT registration number in the invoice header (include it in a note at the bottom if the client asks)  
✓ When you issue the real invoice, the VAT amount must match what you charged, and you show your VAT number on the real invoice  

### Example wording for VAT note on proforma (VAT registered)

> **VAT Note:** This is not a VAT invoice. VAT shown above (£1,200) is an estimate based on the current standard rate (20%). The actual VAT invoice will be issued when the deposit or final invoice is sent. Our VAT registration number is GB 234 5678 90 (provided for reference only).

### If you are NOT VAT registered

Do not show VAT on the proforma. Write "Not applicable" or leave the VAT line blank.

You do not need to include a disclaimer about VAT if you are not VAT registered, but it can help to add a note:

> **VAT Note:** This business is not VAT registered. No VAT will be charged.

### Can the client reclaim VAT from a proforma invoice?

No. A proforma is not a VAT invoice. The client cannot use it to reclaim VAT. They must wait for the real invoice (deposit or final) before they can reclaim VAT.

**Source:** [GOV.UK — VAT invoices](https://www.gov.uk/vat-businesses/vat-invoices)

## Common mistakes that confuse clients (and delay payment)

**Calling it an invoice when it is a proforma.** Label it "Proforma Invoice" clearly at the top. If you call it "Invoice" and add a note saying "this is not an invoice," that is confusing.

**Including payment terms on a proforma.** Do not write "Net 14" or "Due 30 September 2026" on a proforma. It is not a request for payment, so there is no due date. Use a validity date instead ("Valid until 30 September 2026").

**Adding bank details to a proforma.** The client should not pay a proforma. Do not include your sort code and account number on it. That goes on the real invoice.

**Not explaining what happens next.** Always include a note on the proforma explaining that this is for approval, and a deposit invoice or final invoice will follow. Otherwise, clients sit and wait for you to tell them what to do.

**Editing the proforma after the client approves it.** Do not change the proforma reference number or amounts after the client has approved it. If the scope changes, issue a revised proforma with a new reference (e.g. PROFORMA-2026-045-REV1). If no changes, issue a fresh invoice with a new number.

**Treating a proforma like a quote.** A proforma is a fixed estimate. If you send a proforma for £5,000 and then invoice £6,000, the client will push back. Lock the price before you send the proforma.

## When you do not need a proforma invoice — skip it and invoice faster

Most UK freelancers working with small businesses, startups, or direct clients do not need proforma invoices. You only need them when:

✓ The client explicitly asks for a proforma  
✓ You are working with corporate procurement or public sector  
✓ The client has a multi-step approval process  
✓ The client needs documentation to get budget sign-off  

**For most projects, skip the proforma and go straight to a deposit invoice.** It is faster, simpler, and you get paid sooner.

If a client asks for a proforma and you are not sure why, ask:

> "Do you need a proforma for internal approval, or are you ready to proceed with a deposit invoice?"

If they are ready to proceed, send the deposit invoice. If they need approval, send the proforma first, then the deposit invoice once they confirm.

## Decision checklist — should I send a proforma invoice for this project?

Use this checklist to decide whether you need a proforma or can skip it.

**Send a proforma invoice if:**

✓ The client asked for a 'proforma' by name  
✓ The client is a corporate or public sector organisation with procurement processes  
✓ The client needs internal sign-off and wants a formal document before committing  
✓ You want to give a fixed estimate that looks official but is not yet a payment demand  
✓ The project will not start for weeks and the client needs documentation now  

**Skip the proforma and send a deposit invoice (or quote) if:**

✓ The client is a small business, startup, or sole trader  
✓ The client is ready to start and has budget approved  
✓ The client has not mentioned needing a proforma  
✓ You want to get paid faster (proforma → approval → deposit invoice is slower than just sending a deposit invoice)  
✓ The project is under £1,000 and informal  

**Bottom line:** If in doubt, ask the client what they need. Most clients who ask for a proforma know exactly why they need one. Most clients who do not ask for one do not need one.
