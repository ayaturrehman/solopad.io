Choose freelance time tracking software by checking what happens after you log an hour. You may need a personal time report, a client-ready timesheet or a route to preparing an invoice. Those are different requirements.

I build SoloPad. This guide compares published plans and practical trial checks. It does not claim that every tool was tested on real client work.

## Decide what the time record is for

For fixed-fee work, time records help you compare the effort with your quote. Include meetings, revisions and admin so the effective hourly rate reflects the full job.

For hourly billing, agree what counts as billable work and how you will report it. Record a short task description while the work is fresh. A useful entry explains what was done without exposing unrelated client information.

A time log can support a billing conversation. It does not guarantee payment or resolve a contractual dispute by itself.

## Test the complete billing workflow

Create a sample project and record three entries: one billable task, one non-billable task and one entry that needs correcting. Then prepare the timesheet or report you would send to a client.

Check the steps needed to produce an invoice. Confirm that rates, descriptions and approved hours are correct. Do not assume an integrated tool automatically converts every time entry into an invoice.

## Make the habit easy to keep

Track work consistently and review entries before sending a bill. Agree any rounding rule with the client in advance. A [timesheet template](/blog/timesheet-template-uk) can help you settle the fields you need before choosing software.
